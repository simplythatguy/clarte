## Stable actions and recoverable interaction

- **Keep actions in predictable locations across visual states.** HIG connects consistent placement and behavior with learning, confidence, and preserving context. Clarte: keep letter groups and repeated actions anchored; feedback must not move, enlarge, or introduce an independent action into an occupied backend quadrant. Reserve genuinely unowned quadrants for reading, or use the contract's independent confirmation approach; an empty visual strip is not itself safe. Values: Clarte's existing 2 rows × 4 columns, Q1–Q8, at most one action per cell; these are project constraints, not HIG findings. [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Provide a simple, visible route for every essential operation.** HIG favors uncomplicated repeatable interactions and onscreen equivalents for gestures. Clarte: correction, leaving a view, navigation, and stopping speech must not depend on swipes, dragging, hover, a keyboard, spoken commands, or an available physical switch. Each gaze-selectable equivalent must own its cells. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Separate the size of a visible symbol from the space needed to select its action.** HIG treats target size and spacing as complementary protections against errors. Clarte: a small, calm glyph may label a large quadrant-owned action; native point minimums cannot justify adding more actions inside a quadrant. Values: HIG default/minimum control dimensions are iOS/iPadOS 44×44/28×28 pt, macOS 28×28/20×20 pt, and visionOS 60×60/28×28 pt; suggested padding is about 12 pt around bezel-bearing controls or 24 pt around visible edges without a bezel. Layout separately suggests visionOS button centers at least 60 pt apart. These are native reference values, not validated Clarte dimensions or CSS-pixel conversions. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Leave essential information available until the person dismisses it.** HIG warns that timed disappearance disadvantages people who process information or traverse controls slowly. Clarte: predictions, errors, recovery options, and recording results must not expire while someone reads; do not introduce a countdown that speaks a draft automatically. HIG supplies no Clarte dwell duration. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Make recovery preserve the person's work and control.** HIG treats reversal and return to a previous state as foundations of agency. Clarte: make an accidental group selection or word acceptance repairable without clearing the draft; preserve composition when leaving a temporary view; let the person control speech start and stop. Speech already heard cannot be taken back, so draft correction is not a substitute for deliberate release. [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Show what happened and what is currently possible.** HIG calls for clear availability and change feedback. Clarte: distinguish looking at a target, building dwell, accepting input, recording, paused recording, speaking, and blocked tracking. Keep the feedback local and restrained so an acknowledgment does not compete with the message. [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles)

- **Keep standard assistive navigation working alongside gaze.** HIG recommends testing keyboard, screen-reader, and switch-based access and avoiding system-shortcut overrides. Clarte: expose meaningful controls and a logical keyboard route for people or partners who use those methods, while ensuring the primary gaze route remains independently complete. Supporting another method does not establish that a particular person can perform it. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

## Text and enlargement

- **Allow substantial text enlargement without losing the task.** HIG recommends user-adjustable text and icon size. Clarte: support browser text enlargement/zoom and an accessible size accommodation where needed; test the draft, predictions, labels, and error recovery together rather than enlarging only headings. Values: HIG recommends enlargement of at least 200% for the relevant non-watch platforms. This is not a claim that implementing a size control alone establishes web conformance. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Use readable type weights and treat native small-text limits as references.** HIG favors regular through bold weights because thin strokes become hard to perceive, especially at small sizes. Clarte: avoid light-weight functional text; choose sizes for the person's actual viewing distance and low-vision needs rather than copying a desktop minimum. Values: native default/minimum text sizes are macOS 13/10 pt and iOS/iPadOS 17/11 pt; these are not prescribed Clarte browser font sizes. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

- **Express a small, consistent type hierarchy.** HIG recommends few typefaces and preserving relative emphasis during size changes. Clarte: let the current message dominate, with actions and supporting status subordinate; do not make settings labels compete with the composition. Keep the hierarchy recognizable when the person enlarges text. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

- **Make meaningful icons grow with text and expose full message content.** HIG warns about crowding, overlap, and truncation at large sizes. Clarte: scale recognition-critical glyphs with labels, wrap the person's words, and provide a gaze-reachable way to read any remaining content. Any stacking or change in visible actions must retain valid backend ownership and reading safety. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

- **Give multiline text enough line spacing to keep one's place.** HIG recommends more leading for wide or long passages and rejects compressed leading for longer multiline blocks. Clarte: use comfortable spacing in the partner transcript and composed reply instead of shrinking or tightly packing them to preserve decorative chrome. Values: avoid tight leading at 3 or more lines. As native examples rather than web tokens, the iOS default body specification is 17 pt type with 22 pt leading; its largest listed accessibility body is 53/62 pt. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

- **Check legibility at the distances and sizes people will actually use.** HIG calls for testing across viewing conditions, platforms, and scales. Clarte: inspect enlarged text and ordinary use from the gaze user's seated position and the partner's viewing position on supported screens; a close-up design-tool view does not establish comfortable reading or gaze accuracy. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)

## Color, contrast, and appearance

- **Pair every meaningful color with a recognizable noncolor cue.** HIG says color cannot be the only way to distinguish objects, state, or interactivity. Clarte: keep letter ranges printed on G1–G4, preserve stable positions, and distinguish recording, selection, errors, and availability with labels, shapes, or marks plus accessible state information. Four different hues alone do not make the groups distinguishable. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Give each functional color a consistent job.** HIG warns that reusing an interaction color for unrelated inert text can mislead people. Clarte: keep group identity, current selection, recording, and blocking errors semantically distinct; make any colored element answer a concrete recognition or status need. Check cultural interpretations when localizing. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion)

- **Measure foreground/background contrast in the real state combinations.** HIG recommends contrast calculators and checks in both appearances. Clarte: check the message, inactive labels, selected targets, dwell feedback, and errors against their actual surfaces; a compliant idle label says nothing about its selected state. Values: the Dark Mode page calls for at least 4.5:1 and suggests 7:1 for custom foreground/background pairs, particularly small text. Accessibility's native Inspector table also gives 3:1 for specified larger/bold categories; its simplified table must not become a blanket web exception for all bold text. No APCA threshold or conformance result is provided here. [Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Define colors by their role and provide accessible appearance variants.** HIG's semantic colors adapt to context, and it cautions against using a separator or secondary-label color for a different function. Clarte: define browser tokens for message text, supporting text, surfaces, focus, errors, and group cues; specify each required appearance deliberately instead of copying native RGB values. Values: HIG requests light, dark, increased-contrast light, and increased-contrast dark variants for custom colors. The supplied color tables contain no numeric swatch values to reuse. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)

- **Respect the person's appearance preference and design each palette deliberately.** HIG expects system appearance changes to work during use and explains that dark colors are not simply inverted light colors. Clarte: use the browser's system-preference equivalent as an initial behavior; preserve message content, action locations, and usable states if appearance changes. The source does not establish a universally preferable AAC theme or a theme that improves webcam tracking. [Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)

- **Verify text, icons, and images through combined accessibility appearances.** HIG notes that dark appearance, increased contrast, and reduced transparency can interact unexpectedly. Clarte: inspect their available browser equivalents separately and together; use alternative icon/image treatments only where one asset fails. Ensure reduced transparency does not remove a selection cue or merge content with controls. [Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)

- **Judge color and brightness on real displays in representative lighting.** HIG explains that ambient light, display profiles, and translucent backgrounds change perceived color and contrast; spatial guidance also warns against isolated very bright moving content against darkness. Clarte: test dim and bright rooms and ordinary laptop displays, and avoid glaring/flashing focus feedback. Keep the claim limited to visual comfort until Clarte-specific tracking and user evidence exists. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)

## Screen structure and materials

- **Let hierarchy reveal the current task immediately.** HIG puts important content first and uses hierarchy to make next actions recognizable. Clarte: make the current reply or current recording task the major thing; show essential phase information without turning the partner transcript, settings, and composition into equal competing panels. Reading direction informs ordering, subject to fixed physical target ownership. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles)

- **Use alignment and whitespace to show relationships before adding containers.** HIG explains that aligned items appear related and negative space can group them. Clarte: build one coherent composer; let letter ranges, message, and their controls align consistently. A bezel or card around every target is not required to communicate the underlying grid. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Disclose secondary capabilities when needed without removing access to them.** HIG recommends menus or subsequent views to reduce initial choices and says functionality should remain available as space changes. Clarte: put infrequent settings on a gaze-operable secondary view with a return path; every menu entry, page control, and close action still needs valid quadrant ownership. Progressive disclosure is not permission to hide essential recovery behind hover. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Adapt to available space and inspect the extremes.** HIG bases layout on available width/height, safe areas, text size, and localization rather than device names. Clarte: check the smallest and largest supported browser viewports, zoom, long translations, and partially obscured windows; keep critical actions visible and validate the fixed-grid allocation in each supported state. Mac-specific advice cautions against critical content at a window's bottom edge, which merits visibility checks for Clarte's lower action row. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Use a surface only when it clarifies function and preserves legibility.** HIG limits Liquid Glass to a functional layer, warns that overuse adds distraction, and notes that more opaque materials help fine text contrast. Clarte: transfer the hierarchy principle, not the glass styling; keep message content visually quiet, and ensure an open menu or overlay is distinguishable and readable against every background. Decorative translucency and nested material panels earn no automatic place in a web AAC composer. [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials)

## Motion and feedback

- **Keep frequent-input feedback brief and free of unnecessary waiting.** HIG discourages custom animation on repeated interactions, asks for precise feedback, and says animation should be cancellable where possible. Clarte: acknowledge group entry and correction in place without a bounce, traveling token, or completion delay on every letter. The page supplies no general millisecond duration or dwell threshold for this app. [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

- **Avoid repeated and peripheral movement that pulls the eyes away.** HIG highlights distraction and discomfort from peripheral movement and sustained oscillation. Clarte: avoid pulsing controls, animated side decorations, and moving prediction labels; meaningful recording status can remain steady. Values: visionOS guidance singles out oscillation around 0.2 Hz as particularly sensitive; this is spatial comfort advice, not a validated browser frequency threshold or a reason to certify other frequencies as safe. [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

- **Make motion optional while retaining the same information.** HIG says motion cannot be the only carrier of important information and recommends reducing bounce, zoom, depth changes, and repetitive movement when requested. Clarte: honor the browser's reduced-motion preference; retain a clear static state and accessible announcement when an animation is removed. Optional audio is supplementary and cannot replace visible feedback. [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Keep a stationary frame of reference through transitions.** HIG recommends stable reference areas and suggests fading instead of unnecessary object travel. Clarte: retain action anchors during phase changes and update the message in place; where a transition is useful, a restrained fade may avoid chasing content. This adaptation must preserve the distinction between reading and selecting; a fade alone does not prevent an unintended dwell. [Motion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/motion)

## Screen-reader and multimodal access

- **Give every important control a precise, current accessible name.** HIG requires labels for custom elements and updated descriptions as functionality changes. Clarte: name icon-only actions by what they do and expose current recording/availability state; synchronize the name when an action changes from Record to Pause. Backend identifiers such as Q6 are not adequate public labels. [VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)

- **Make structure and reading order available without relying on visual proximity.** HIG recommends unique page titles, meaningful headings, and explicit grouping and ordering. Clarte: use semantic browser structure so a transcript stays with its speaker, a candidate with its acceptance control, and an error with its affected function. Keep navigation order consistent with the active language without changing physical quadrant IDs. [VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)

- **Report meaningful content changes to assistive technology.** HIG says unexpected visible changes invalidate a person's mental map unless the changes are conveyed. Clarte: expose prediction acceptance, errors, phase changes, and recording state changes through appropriate browser accessibility mechanisms; avoid flooding announcements with each noisy gaze sample. The announcement policy is a Clarte interpretation, not a tested result. [VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover)

- **Keep meaningful content available through another perceivable channel.** HIG recommends descriptions for informative images, exclusion of decoration from screen-reader navigation, and text alternatives for essential audio. Clarte: keep the spoken reply and recorded-partner transcript visible as appropriate to the flow; describe useful calibration illustrations, and hide decorative art from accessibility traversal. A sound must not be the only confirmation or failure cue. [VoiceOver](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/voiceover), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

## Writing, inclusion, and dignity

- **Use short, consistent action words that describe the result.** HIG favors plain verbs, stable terminology, and wording appropriate to the input method. Clarte: use labels such as Record, Pause, Speak, and Delete where those actions exist; do not substitute unexplained backend abbreviations or tell a gaze user to click. Keep labels consistent across visible UI and accessible names. [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing), [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion)

- **State errors near the problem and give a usable recovery step.** HIG recommends actionable, blame-free errors instead of vague failures or playful interjections. Clarte: make a tracking or permission failure name what is blocked and present a gaze-reachable remedy when feasible; preserve the draft. An error's recovery action must fit the action budget, not appear as a tiny extra link within an occupied cell. [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)

- **Make empty or waiting states communicate the useful next action.** HIG says an empty view should make the next step apparent and must not be the sole home for essential information that later disappears. Clarte: expose the relevant start action or concise status without adding empty-count badges, decorative filler, or an instructional paragraph above the composer. Privacy boundaries and necessary recovery information remain available when content arrives. [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)

- **Label fields clearly and use examples only where they remove ambiguity.** HIG recommends labels, concise hints, and adjacent corrective feedback. Clarte: keep persistent names for actual settings fields; do not rely on placeholder text that vanishes after entry, or repeat an obvious label in a helper sentence. The one obvious composition input can follow the owner's permitted placeholder exception. [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing)

- **Do not infer a person's abilities or goals from one disability.** HIG treats disability as varied and cautions against excluding people by assuming they cannot want or use an experience. Clarte: dysarthria does not establish a cognitive impairment, literacy level, ability to speak commands, or availability of hand movement. Keep the gaze task complete, and make accommodations responsive to the person rather than imposing a stereotype. [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)

- **Address people respectfully and make representations recognizable to them.** HIG recommends direct, plain language, avoids stereotypes, and asks writers to respect how a person or community self-identifies. Clarte: use an adult, respectful voice; avoid patronizing praise, ability-based jokes, jargon, and generic figures that unnecessarily imply a gender or a single kind of family, body, or economic situation. The appropriate tone and imagery need user input rather than a diagnosis-based assumption. [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion)

- **Offer learning support without forcing it into routine communication.** HIG recommends stepwise onboarding for newcomers with a way for experienced people to bypass it; its agency principle also favors escape from guided flows. Clarte: optional, task-specific practice or help may support learning, but should not consume every compose screen with explanatory copy. The exact teaching flow remains a proposal for parent integration, subject to minimal-ui and gaze access. [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion), [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles)

## Icons, images, and restrained branding

- **Choose a simple, familiar icon for one clear concept.** HIG warns that detail harms recognition and favors metaphors directly related to the represented action. Clarte: use recognizable symbols for common actions; give an ambiguous action a short visible word rather than expecting every person to decode a novel AAC metaphor. An icon's artwork and its large selection area serve different purposes. [Icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons)

- **Match icon weight and alignment to surrounding text.** HIG asks for consistent detail, stroke weight, perspective, and optical rather than merely geometric centering. Clarte: keep functional icons from looking arbitrarily heavier than labels; adjust asymmetric artwork within its stationary target without moving the target itself. The SF system's 9 weights and 3 relative scales illustrate matching emphasis, not a requirement to reproduce that API on the web. [Icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons), [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols)

- **Use coherent symbol variants to make states recognizable.** HIG describes related outline, fill, and slash forms and requires checking the rendering mode against size and background. Clarte: distinguish available, selected, and stopped/muted states with consistent shapes plus accessible status; prefer a restrained monochrome treatment where extra layers or hues reduce clarity. A filled glyph need not become a filled card around every item. [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols)

- **Use scalable vector artwork for functional interface icons.** HIG recommends vector formats so icons remain sharp when scaled. Clarte: use suitable SVG artwork for browser controls and test it at enlarged sizes, rather than stretching a low-resolution bitmap or assuming an Apple system symbol will exist in every browser. Preserve meaningful accessible names. [Icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icons), [Images](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/images)

- **Keep system-symbol usage restrictions separate from visual inspiration.** HIG explicitly excludes SF Symbols and confusingly similar images from app icons, logos, and trademark use, and identifies some Apple-product symbols as noncustomizable. Clarte: do not turn an SF Symbol into the Clarte mark or casually export a restricted symbol as a supposedly unrestricted brand asset; verify the actual terms before choosing a source for shipped web artwork. These notes do not establish a general web redistribution license. [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols)

- **Supply crisp imagery without stretching its proportions.** HIG calls for appropriate image resolution, embedded color profiles, and inspection on actual devices. Clarte: use vector illustrations where useful; supply suitable raster density variants for instructional images and keep them sharp during enlargement without adding decorative image weight to composition. Values: the supplied page distinguishes @1x, @2x, and @3x as 1, 2, and 3 pixels per point in its native 2D examples; these are asset-density references, not gaze geometry. [Images](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/images), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)

- **Let branding defer to the person's message.** HIG limits repeated logos and broad accent-color use because they displace or distract from useful content, and discourages branding on a transient launch screen. Clarte: express identity through a restrained mark, consistent voice, and familiar components; do not spend a reading quadrant on a repeated logo, decorative gradient, or launch animation. [Branding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding)

- **Make the app's identifying mark simple and consistent at small sizes.** HIG favors one strong concept, few shapes, minimal essential text, and the same core features across icon appearances. Clarte: a favicon or install icon should remain recognizable in small browser contexts and alternate backgrounds without relying on a slogan, fine-line detail, a screenshot, or color alone. Native layered-icon formats and platform export sizes do not become web manifest requirements. [App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons)

## Right-to-left language and localization

- **Align text for its reading context and language.** HIG distinguishes short UI text, longer paragraphs, and consistently aligned lists. Clarte: localize alignment of controls and lists, but let long partner transcripts and replies follow their actual language. Values: HIG treats 3 or more lines as a paragraph aligned by its own language; 1–2 line blocks follow the surrounding interface direction. Mixed-language content needs direct inspection. [Right to left](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left)

- **Mirror semantic navigation without reversing physical directions or backend identities.** HIG flips forward/back and ordered progress controls while preserving arrows that mean an actual physical direction. Clarte: localize the page-navigation meaning and relevant progress direction, then validate action placement; Q1–Q8 remain the existing physical grid labels. A general RTL CSS flip must not silently redefine which action a classified quadrant selects. [Right to left](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left)

- **Preserve text and numbers rather than reversing character sequences.** HIG keeps digits within a number in their original order while adapting ordered controls and locale-appropriate number presentation. Clarte: render mixed-script messages, names, phone numbers, and numerical content through appropriate text-direction handling; do not reverse raw strings. Translating the interface does not make the existing Latin G1–G4 decoder support another alphabet. [Right to left](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left)

- **Localize icons and balance script legibility selectively.** HIG flips reading-direction imagery, localizes textual glyphs, and preserves logos, photographs, universal marks, and most real-object orientations. Clarte: check each navigation or text-related symbol instead of mirroring the whole screen indiscriminately; inspect Arabic/Hebrew labels beside Latin group text. Values: HIG suggests about +2 pt for Arabic/Hebrew beside uppercase Latin where visual balance requires it, a native heuristic rather than an automatic CSS adjustment. [Right to left](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/right-to-left), [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols)

## Privacy and trusted access

- **Request each resource when its purpose is apparent and necessary.** HIG discourages premature or excessive access requests but allows launch-time access when essential to the product. Clarte: make the camera request part of entering gaze use, and request the microphone when the person starts recording partner context; do not bundle unrelated permissions. The permission must match the actual implemented need. [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

- **Explain actual collection and use in a brief, specific sentence.** HIG prefers an active, complete explanation of why access is needed over vague claims about improving the experience. Clarte: explain what camera frames, recordings, transcripts, and remote decoding actually do in the delivered system, including any relevant transmission; never label something “on-device” merely because a local UI renders it. Values: HIG recommends sentence case and a final period for native permission-purpose copy. Browser prompt text may not be app-editable. [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

- **Keep the real permission decision clear and verify that people can reach it.** HIG rejects manipulative pre-prompts, fake system requests, and custom buttons labeled as if they grant access; its native pre-alert uses a single Continue/Next action to open the real alert. Clarte: any necessary explanation must clearly lead to the browser's actual permission decision. The app's eight-cell selector does not by itself operate browser chrome, so permission and denial recovery need an independently verified access path; this research did not test one. The native prohibition on a pre-alert Cancel button is not a general web onboarding rule. [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

- **Minimize collection and keep processing local where the feature permits.** HIG links collecting only necessary data and on-device processing with reducing privacy exposure and remote round trips. Clarte: treat raw frames, gaze histories, partner audio/transcripts, and drafts as purposeful data rather than automatic telemetry; document what must leave the browser for the actual model. Local processing is a design direction from HIG, not evidence that the current pipeline is local. [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

- **Use established protections and respect shared-device context.** HIG recommends platform security, rejects plaintext storage of sensitive secure content and custom authentication schemes, and cautions against assuming who is signed in. Clarte: keep sensitive information out of casual plaintext files/logs and use appropriate browser/server security mechanisms where storage or accounts exist; do not equate an open browser profile or OS session with permission to expose a person's drafts or partner recordings. No authentication system or retention policy was selected by this batch. [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)

## Not applicable

No page was wholly inapplicable; each contributed at least one transferable rule above. Omitted material includes Apple API names and entitlements, App Store submission procedures, native document-icon canvases, tvOS overscan/grid specifications and parallax-layer counts, watch-size tables, visionOS spatial media and camera specifics, location-sharing controls, and native app-icon packaging. These do not describe Clarte's current browser surface. The full typography tables were read, but most platform style/tracking entries are not suitable browser AAC tokens.

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- Native touch/pointer dimensions and visionOS spacing are reference values; they cannot override the fixed eight-cell action budget or prove noisy webcam-gaze usability. [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- Typography/layout suggestions to stack controls or reduce columns can move actions across cell boundaries; enlarged/localized states require ownership checks rather than blindly following reflow examples. [Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout)
- HIG's tvOS scaling/parallax focus and expressive symbol effects conflict with stable gaze targets and a calm repeated-input task; recognition feedback must preserve geometry and avoid attention capture. [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [Images](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/images), [SF Symbols](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sf-symbols)
- HIG permits onboarding instruction, settings explanations, and educational empty-state copy; minimal-ui rejects routine explanatory prose. Optional learning support is a proposal, and labels, errors, blocked reasons, and consent boundaries remain necessary. [Writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing), [Inclusion](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/inclusion)
- HIG offers container shapes, separators, rich materials, and brand color within content; minimal-ui limits wrappers and decorative color. Transfer grouping and hierarchy without prescribing boxes, glass, repeated logos, or colored fills on every group. [Layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), [Branding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/branding)
- HIG discourages an app-specific appearance setting; a future individual low-vision/light-sensitivity accommodation might need one. This batch leaves that choice open and does not replace the existing theme research with a new universal default. [Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode), [Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility)
