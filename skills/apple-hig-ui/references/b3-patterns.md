## Feedback and waiting

- **Keep feedback beside the thing it describes and make it perceptible in more than one way.** Local status avoids leaving the task; redundant visual, textual, and optional audible cues accommodate different access needs. Clarte: distinguish looking, pending selection, accepted input, recording, and speaking within the active component; expose status text to assistive technology and never rely on color or a sound alone. [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)
- **Match interruption to the consequence.** Routine status can be passive; critical, actionable information and unexpected irreversible loss deserve stronger intervention. Clarte: keep ordinary prediction and save status quiet; interrupt for a consequential problem the person can address, rather than requiring confirmation after every reversible group entry. Speaking needs separate deliberate release because the listener cannot unhear it. [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)
- **Explain a blocked action where the person encounters it.** A command that cannot run needs a reason and a useful next step. Clarte: a failed microphone request, unavailable voice, or empty message must not produce a silent no-op; give a short state such as “Microphone blocked” with a reachable recovery action. Minimal presentation must not hide the failure. [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)
- **Show a useful, stable interface before background work finishes.** Apple recommends displaying content promptly and allowing other work during loading. Clarte: render the composer and current groups immediately while predictions load; preserve input and target positions as results arrive, and keep independent editing available. Do not add moving entertainment or cycling tips to fill the wait. [Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading)
- **Represent progress honestly.** Use measurable progress when duration is known and an ongoing-work state when it is not. Clarte: keep a small loading state by the candidate area; never fabricate a percentage or completion estimate for an unpredictable model call, and do not require the person to keep watching an animated indicator. Values: Apple says to indicate loading that lasts more than a moment or two; this is qualitative guidance, not a measured webcam timing threshold. [Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading), [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)

## Starting and learning

- **Resume the person's work without replaying startup ceremony.** Apple emphasizes fast launch, visual continuity, and restoration of granular previous state. Clarte: restore the draft, pending groups, and relevant place after a restart without a branding interstitial; separately show whether gaze and recording are ready rather than restoring an old active selection, starting speech, or resuming capture merely because a draft was restored. [Launching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/launching)
- **Teach one real action through a safe, optional trial.** Interactivity aids learning more than a large memorization burden, and a skipped tutorial should remain available without recurring at every launch. Clarte: offer a practice group selection, correction, and speech preview in a safe learning context; keep the tutorial distinct from any tracking setup genuinely required for access. [Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **Postpone setup that is not needed for the next useful action.** Defaults and contextual permission requests reduce initial burden. Clarte: defer voice personalization and account setup; request camera access when gaze setup needs it and microphone access when the person begins the partner-recording function, with a concise purpose. Do not bundle unrelated permissions into a prerequisite wizard. [Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding)
- **Make help relevant to the current task and input method.** Help should address the person's actual action, use consistent language, and be easy to avoid or dismiss. Clarte: provide an on-demand explanation of accepting a prediction or changing a dwell setting, using gaze terms and a safe demonstration; mouse hover, a keyboard shortcut, or a small tooltip cannot be the sole route to required help. [Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help)
- **Keep optional hints small enough to act on immediately.** Apple recommends brief, targeted tips and concise tooltips rather than instructions for complex procedures. Clarte: put essential action names in controls; put any necessary longer instruction in optional help, not persistent subtitles. Values: tips suit features requiring no more than 3 actions and generally use 1–2 sentences; Apple gives once per 24 hours as an example cadence, not a requirement; macOS/visionOS tooltip guidance targets a maximum of 60–75 characters. These are content budgets, not gaze-reading deadlines. [Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help)

## Modes and recovery

- **Use a modal interruption only for a short, clearly named task with an obvious exit.** Modal views suspend the parent context and add dismissal work; nested modal hierarchies increase memory load. Clarte: an essential confirmation should offer a small choice set, a visible cancel route, and preservation of the draft. Do not build settings navigation inside a stack of dialogs. Values: Apple says to dismiss one modal before another and never show more than 1 alert at once. [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality)
- **Make a temporary mode own all of its input.** A modal view prevents interaction with the parent, so obscured actions must not remain active. Clarte: when help, confirmation, or a feature page replaces the main task, deactivate the previous actions and clear pending dwell before assigning the new quadrant map; an exit control must itself have valid backend ownership. The fixed grid and dwell reset are Clarte adaptations. [Modality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/modality)
- **Make the next undo predictable and show its result.** People can repeatedly undo unintended changes when they cannot see what a command did. Clarte: distinguish removing the last pending group, replacing an accepted word, and clearing the draft; name the relevant operation and show the repaired content without moving it into another action's area. [Undo and redo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/undo-and-redo)
- **Support successive reversible repairs, including repair of a mistaken correction.** Apple recommends multiple undo levels and sometimes a grouped reversal; corrections should also be correctable. Clarte: let the person restore an accidentally removed word or draft without recomposing everything, while making the scope of any batch reversal clear. Undoing draft text cannot recall already spoken audio. [Undo and redo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/undo-and-redo), [Machine learning — Corrections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)

## Data entry, search, and settings

- **Ask for less input and offer suitable choices before demanding typing.** Existing permitted data, clear labels, and reasonable defaults reduce tedious entry. Clarte: reuse a chosen voice or personal phrase and offer simple relevant settings choices; page choices within the fixed quadrant budget instead of presenting a dense native picker. Preserve a route to novel wording rather than limiting communication to suggested phrases. [Entering data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data)
- **Validate entry near the error and make requirements clear.** Early validation avoids the cost of revisiting a completed form, and a person needs to understand why they cannot continue. Clarte: show a short field-specific issue before leaving setup; do not silently disable Continue, mislabel an optional field as required, or silently replace the person's words as if a prediction were validation. [Entering data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/entering-data), [Feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback)
- **Give search one clear home and an explicit scope, with privacy controls for history.** Suggestions and recent queries can reduce typing, but displaying history can expose private interests. Clarte: if searching saved phrases, show that scope in the control, keep results within a gaze-operable view, and let the person hide or clear history before a partner sees it. Do not introduce a second always-visible search area in the composer. [Searching](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/searching)
- **Keep immediate task options close and infrequent preferences one step away.** Separating task-specific adjustments from general settings preserves context and reduces search. Clarte: pause, stop, or a currently needed access adjustment must remain reachable during communication; rarely changed voice and account preferences belong in a quiet settings route. Apple’s advice to reduce setting count must not remove necessary accessibility capabilities. [Settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings)
- **Respect system preferences and avoid duplicate controls with ambiguous scope.** Apple advises using systemwide accessibility, appearance, and authentication settings rather than implying a local copy changes the whole device. Clarte: honor available browser/system motion and appearance preferences, and make the scope of any necessary app-specific dwell or presentation override clear. This does not assume the browser exposes every native setting. [Settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings)

## Focus and interruptions

- **Keep nonessential notifications and feedback requests out of active conversation.** Notification urgency should be accurate, ratings should wait for engagement and natural breaks, and feedback should be voluntary. Clarte: do not interrupt composing or speaking for a rating, feature announcement, or routine completion message; make unwanted notifications easy to turn off. Values: the ratings page suggests at least 1–2 weeks between repeat requests; its cap of 3 prompts per 365 days is native StoreKit behavior, not a web allowance or proposed Clarte schedule. [Managing notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-notifications), [Ratings and reviews](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ratings-and-reviews)
- **Let the person choose full screen and retain every essential control there.** Focus benefits from fewer distractions, but entering or leaving full screen should remain the person's choice. Clarte: preserve gaze-reachable stop, cancel, navigation, and exit functions; an edge hover or Escape key alone is not an adequate exit for gaze-only use. [Going full screen](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/going-full-screen)
- **Preserve spatial continuity when the viewport or presentation changes.** Apple recommends subtle layout adjustments rather than jarring changes between windowed and full-screen modes. Clarte: the backend remains 2 rows × 4 columns with at most one action per cell, and controls must retain valid ownership at each supported viewport. Suspend selection during a geometry transition and validate the new layout before rearming; this guard is a project-specific application, not a HIG timing prescription. [Going full screen](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/going-full-screen), [Multitasking](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/multitasking)
- **Save context before a task switch and resume active participation safely.** Apple recommends pausing attention-dependent tasks while preserving context and finishing user-initiated background work that needs no further input. Clarte: preserve the draft and let an initiated decode finish, but clear pending dwell on deactivation and require a safe return to selection. A late model response must not commit or speak; merely glancing away is not the same event as switching apps. [Multitasking](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/multitasking)

## Preserving work, sharing, and accounts

- **Preserve work automatically without making retention a hidden promise.** Autosaving reduces the need for explicit save actions, while unsaved changes must remain apparent when saving is unavailable. Clarte: protect expensive-to-compose drafts across accidental navigation and report persistence failures. Decide sensitive transcript retention separately; “autosave” does not grant permission for indefinite storage or server sync. [File management](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/file-management), [Generative AI — Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Provide discrete alternatives to every dragging operation.** Apple explicitly notes that dragging can be inconvenient or impossible. Clarte: any supported phrase import, reordering, or text move needs an equivalent sequence of gaze-selectable actions; do not require precision dragging, a continuous hold, or hover-triggered spring loading. A familiar native dialog is not proof that its contents satisfy the quadrant model. [Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop), [File management](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/file-management)
- **Show what a content transfer will do and make mistakes reversible.** Copy and move have different consequences; slow or failed transfers need clear feedback and accidental placement should be undoable. Clarte: if phrase or text transfers are supported, keep the source intact unless a move is explicit, identify the destination, and show success or failure without silently deleting the original. [Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop)
- **Keep sharing intentional and make its current scope visible.** Apple emphasizes succinct permissions, a small set of access choices, and a visible indication that content is shared. Clarte: distinguish a private working draft from content shown to a partner; if remote sharing exists, make the audience and read/edit rights clear and manageable. A nearby partner does not automatically have editing or retention permission. Native Share and Collaboration buttons need not become two permanent Clarte controls. [Collaboration and sharing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/collaboration-and-sharing)
- **Avoid account barriers to useful communication and minimize authentication entry.** Apple recommends requiring an account only when core functionality needs one, delaying sign-in, and using available low-entry authentication methods. Clarte: keep any feasible local communication path available before cloud sign-in; label the actual supported method and verify that its system interaction is usable with the person's access method. A passkey or a second device is not automatically accessible to a gaze-only user. [Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **Make account deletion discoverable and explain its completion state.** The account guidance distinguishes deletion from deactivation and asks for a clear initiation path and completion information. Clarte: if accounts are provided, expose deletion in account settings with a concise account-specific consequence, expected timing, and status; do not force a search through policy pages or imply local calibration data was removed when only the account was disabled. This is an interface principle, not a legal-compliance assessment. [Managing accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts)
- **Use a chart only when it answers a real comparison or trend question.** Charts attract attention and can overwhelm; labels, accessible values, and progressively revealed detail aid comprehension. Clarte: a single readiness or calibration result is usually a short status, while any requested diagnostic trend belongs in a dedicated view with meaningful labels and an accessible text equivalent. Do not decorate the communication surface with prediction-confidence dashboards. [Charting data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charting-data)

## Audio and help video

- **Separate deliberate speech from optional interface sounds.** Apple distinguishes explicitly initiated playback from incidental sounds and requires important information to have a channel other than sound. Clarte: the person chooses when the message is spoken; selection ticks and success sounds remain optional and visually redundant, without masking the partner or synthesised speech. Silence of interface effects must not be mistaken for a broken speaker. [Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)
- **Handle interruptions and output changes without surprising the person.** Apple says headphone disconnection should pause playback, system volume should govern output, and automatic resumption depends on context; it specifically warns against restarting a microphone without the person's knowledge. Clarte: provide a reachable stop action, preserve the interrupted message, and use deliberate resumption after a privacy-sensitive interruption. Do not resume recording or replay speech merely on tab return; check what route/interruption events the web runtime can actually detect before claiming support. [Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)
- **Keep instructional media optional, controllable, and visually secondary.** Familiar playback behavior, original aspect ratio, unobscured controls, and user-chosen playback support comfort and orientation. Clarte: a short calibration demonstration should have gaze-reachable play, stop, and return actions without covering the active targets; choosing a standard video player does not establish gaze accessibility. Transfer the principle of brief clips, not watchOS-specific codec, resolution, or wrist-fatigue limits. [Playing video](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-video)

## Language-model word predictions

- **Distinguish an essential decoder from optional generative enhancement.** Apple's keyboard-suggestion example is complementary because the ordinary keyboard remains usable; a critical dependency requires stronger reliability and failure planning. Clarte: G1–G4 input may depend on decoding, so do not claim that hiding suggestions leaves normal typing intact. Preserve entered groups on outages, and design and verify an accessible alternative such as exact spelling or saved phrases before promising continued communication without the model. [Machine learning — The role of machine learning](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning), [Generative AI](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Identify generated suggestions without presenting them as the person's authored message.** Transparent AI use lets people understand and knowingly choose the feature. Clarte: make candidate versus accepted text distinguishable within the composer, and explain capability and limitations through concise optional help or an essential boundary notice. Do not add technical labels or a disclaimer to every word. [Generative AI — Transparency](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Keep acceptance and speech under deliberate human control.** Apple requires agency, rejection/reversion, and confirmation before consequential actions that are difficult to undo. Clarte: looking at, reading, ranking, or receiving a candidate must not commit it or speak it; provide a deliberate release of reviewed content, with rejection or repair still available. A high model score does not waive this boundary. This is an AAC application of the HIG, not an Apple dwell-selection specification. [Generative AI — Best practices, Inputs, Outputs](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Constrain generation to the person's expressed intent and known context.** Apple warns that plausible outputs can invent facts and that assumptions about personal attributes can introduce stereotypes. Clarte: use the person's groups and permitted partner context to suggest wording, not to invent new personal facts, beliefs, relationships, or an intended reply. In testing, inspect whether negation, names, time, tone, and disability-specific terms survive; fluent wording is insufficient evidence of fidelity. [Generative AI — Inputs, Models and datasets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Offer cheap guided repair while preserving a route to original wording.** Guided alternatives often cost less than freeform repair, but both can be necessary, and a correction should take effect visibly and remain correctable. Clarte: let the person try an alternative word, remove a pending group, replace a word, or continue toward exact spelling without rebuilding unaffected clauses. These are distinct repairs; selecting another prediction is not a substitute for entering a word the model never offers. [Machine learning — Corrections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning), [Generative AI — Outputs](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Evaluate the saved effort against the full cost of handling mistakes.** Apple says automation loses value when correcting it is harder than doing the task directly, and corrections cannot compensate for poor output quality. Clarte: count candidate reading, choosing, cycling, repair, and resuming speech alongside group selections before claiming a prediction policy is easier or faster. This is a proposed evaluation criterion; this reading measured no speed or fatigue benefit. [Machine learning — Corrections, Mistakes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Offer a small set of distinct, legible choices and put a genuinely likely choice first.** Apple recommends diversity, few enough options to avoid overload, and clear differences, preferably without scrolling. Clarte: respect each screen's available quadrant owners and reading rest zones; stage or page alternatives when necessary instead of squeezing several actionable suggestions into a cell. Ranking first must not mean accepted by default. Values: the HIG supplies no universal candidate count; Clarte's fixed 2×4 grid permits at most 8 distinct actionable owners before allocating rest zones, with spanning actions consuming multiple cells. [Machine learning — Multiple options](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Update predictions at a cadence the person can understand without changing a choice during selection.** Apple contrasts immediate typing suggestions with constantly changing recommendations that rush evaluation. Clarte: new deliberate group input can request updated candidates, but provide a stable review state and freeze its candidate during explicit review/confirmation and any pending dwell; a late response must not change the word beneath an already armed accept action. Do not infer that reading is finished merely from the gaze trace. Reset selection when the available choice changes. The exact freeze/rearm policy needs Clarte testing. [Machine learning — Implicit feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Validate confidence before using it to rank, hide, or explain suggestions.** Apple explicitly says model confidence need not correlate with result quality and advises against communicating it when that relationship is unknown. Clarte: do not show an unsupported “97% correct” badge or equate a top probability with the person's intent; use validated ranking or actionable uncertainty instead. Suppressing a weak suggestion must preserve a real way to continue. Values: Apple defines no universal confidence cutoff; its 97% example illustrates an unhelpful presentation, not a recommended threshold. [Machine learning — Confidence](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Make prediction failure recoverable and explain only what is known.** Apple recommends plain-language failure feedback, relevant alternatives, and honest explanation of limitations. Clarte: keep pending groups and committed words when decoding times out, returns no suitable word, or blocks generation; offer a usable retry, repair, or alternative entry route. Do not invent a technical cause, make the person repeatedly submit the same failed request, or imply a fallback exists before it has been implemented and verified. [Generative AI — Outputs](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai), [Machine learning — Limitations](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Make personal-context use specific, minimal, and controllable.** Apple asks for permission, clear data-use/storage disclosure, minimization, and opt-out, particularly for server processing or improvement. Clarte: disclose which recordings, transcripts, personal words, and usage data leave the device, where they persist, and whether they train a model; collect only what the chosen feature needs. Permission to use context for this reply is not blanket permission to retain it or train on it, and a partner-visible screen must not unexpectedly surface private past context. [Generative AI — Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai), [Machine learning — Implicit feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Test the actual communication task across varied people and difficult inputs, then retest model changes.** Apple calls for representative, licensed data, checks for bias, ambiguous and sensitive inputs, and evaluation after updates. Clarte: test personal names, uncommon vocabulary, dialect and register, noisy group sequences, refusals, and sensitive everyday messages; examine authorship and correction burden as well as fluent output. Keep data provenance and consent, and do not treat a new model or an attractive prototype as proof of improved access. [Generative AI — Models and datasets, Outputs, Continuous improvement](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Make explicit feedback optional, specific, and visibly effective.** Apple prefers clear consequences over vague likes/dislikes, asks for voluntary feedback, and recommends applying and remembering requested changes. Clarte: offer a relevant “Suggest less often” or correction command only when the person seeks it, show its effect, and allow revision; do not make users rate each candidate or add permanent thumbs-up/down controls beside every word. Model-training permission remains separate. [Machine learning — Explicit feedback, Corrections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning), [Generative AI — Continuous improvement](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/generative-ai)
- **Treat behavior as ambiguous evidence rather than a direct report of intent.** Apple warns that indirect signals can be misread, reinforce narrow choices, and change when the UI changes. Clarte: reading time, a drift-induced dwell, accepting a useful sentence, or repeatedly pressing a conveniently located target must not automatically become preference, exact-wording attestation, or training consent. If adaptation is used, consider corroborating signals and recent context, preserve vocabulary beyond frequent suggestions, and separate interaction mistakes from deliberate corrections. [Machine learning — Implicit feedback, Corrections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Use factual explanations of a suggestion only when they help the choice.** Attributions should describe an objective basis without claiming knowledge of emotions or beliefs, and should avoid unnecessary technical statistics. Clarte: an optional explanation can identify that a candidate matches entered groups or permitted conversation context only when that is true; do not say the system “knows what you want.” Keep explanation away from the essential selection path unless it resolves a real ambiguity. [Machine learning — Attribution](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)

## Calibration and tracking limitations

- **Require calibration only for an actual access dependency and keep its demands minimal.** Apple recommends essential information, easy actions, an explicit goal, and visible progress. Clarte: explain the practical camera/gaze requirement briefly, show one current target and progress, and avoid adding model-personalization questions to the tracking setup. Keep the cancel action valid in the quadrant map while selection and reading remain safe. [Machine learning — Calibration](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Respond to stalled calibration with actionable, nonblaming help and confirm only genuine success.** Apple asks for immediate assistance, a clear next step, and explicit completion. Clarte: if evidence supports it, offer a short lighting or camera-position adjustment; allow pause/cancel, and distinguish completed target collection from adequate tracking quality. Do not imply a person failed because of their disability or call unverified tracking ready. [Machine learning — Calibration, Limitations](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- **Avoid unnecessary repeat setup while allowing valid recalibration and removal of saved information.** Apple recommends avoiding repeated personal calibration and letting people update or remove supplied information. Clarte: reuse a saved profile only when a current check supports it; changed posture, lighting, viewport, or drift may justify recalibration. Provide a gaze-reachable way to cancel, update, or clear the profile. “Calibrate once” is not a webcam accuracy guarantee. [Machine learning — Calibration](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)

## Not applicable

- [Live-viewing apps](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-viewing-apps): channel browsing, electronic program guides, broadcast playback, and cloud DVR do not match the stated communication task; their general status/control lessons are already covered by other pages, and autoplay must not transfer to speech or partner recording.
- [Printing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/printing): the brief specifies no printable document or print workflow; printer panels, page setup, and device options supply no distinct current Clarte rule. Do not add a print surface to satisfy this reading exercise.

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- Explanatory onboarding paragraphs, inline tips, and attribution copy can conflict with minimal-ui rule 4; retain short labels, essential boundaries/errors, safe interactive teaching, and optional help rather than persistent instruction prose. [Onboarding](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/onboarding), [Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help), [Machine learning](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Entertaining loading screens, repeated animations, decorative document-launcher accessories, and charts used for personality can conflict with minimal-ui rules 1, 3, and 5 and can attract unintended gaze; preserve useful content and truthful local status without adding those decorative surfaces. [Loading](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/loading), [File management](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/file-management), [Charting data](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charting-data)
- Selecting the first predicted option by default is an optional HIG suggestion, not permission to accept or speak for the person; most-likely-first presentation and deliberate commitment must remain different events. [Machine learning — Multiple options](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Fitting many options on one screen can conflict with one action per backend quadrant and the need for reading rest zones; HIG gives no candidate count that overrides the fixed 2×4 map, and it does not select a single-slot versus staged-choice design for Clarte. [Machine learning — Multiple options](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Updating typing suggestions immediately can conflict with a stable gaze choice; update in response to deliberate input while preventing candidate replacement during review or an armed dwell, and verify the chosen policy. [Machine learning — Implicit feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Avoiding personal calibration more than once cannot override webcam drift, posture changes, or invalid saved profiles; minimize unnecessary repeats while checking present tracking and allowing recalibration. [Machine learning — Calibration](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Standard shortcut-only undo/settings access, hover tooltips, drag-and-drop, and spring loading cannot be the only access path for gaze users; Apple's approximately 3-point drag-image trigger is pointer feedback, not a webcam gaze threshold. [Undo and redo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/undo-and-redo), [Settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings), [Offering help](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/offering-help), [Drag and drop](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/drag-and-drop)
- Hiding essential full-screen controls behind tapping, swiping, or pointer-edge movement can remove the gaze user's exit and recovery routes; visual minimalism must preserve a gaze-operable reveal/exit action. [Going full screen](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/going-full-screen)
- visionOS encouragement to add sound throughout, and media/live-TV autoplay or automatic resume, must not become ambient chatter, automatic AAC speech, or covert microphone resumption; keep optional cues subordinate and communication deliberate. [Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio), [Playing video](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-video), [Live-viewing apps](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-viewing-apps)
- Using implicit feedback to avoid extra user work does not make noisy gaze, reading, or acceptance proof of preference or consent; adaptation needs careful interpretation and must not silently remap the fixed controls. [Machine learning — Implicit feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning)
- Hiding uncertain results and minimizing settings must not remove the only communication or access route; the HIG keyboard example assumes an independent keyboard, whereas grouped decoding may be critical and Clarte requires adjustable access. [Machine learning — Confidence, The role of machine learning](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/machine-learning), [Settings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/settings)
