# b6-technologies-and-rest

Pages read: 36 of 36. All supplied Markdown pages were read in full; none unread. Transferable rules: 31. Pages with no distinct applicable guidance retained: 7.

## Scope and evidence limits

These are transferable findings from the supplied Apple HIG Markdown snapshots, not a proposal to adopt Apple frameworks. Each **Clarte** application is an interpretation for the browser AAC app, not an Apple claim about webcam gaze or evidence of clinical benefit. The fixed 2×4 Q1–Q8 backend, at most one action per owned cell, and real unowned reading/rest cells remain controlling constraints. No browser, participant, gaze-accuracy, or clinical tests were run. Embedded artwork and missing table/image content in the text extracts were not independently inspected. Platform-specific numbers retain their source context; they are not validated Clarte thresholds.

## Glanceable status and stable screens

- **R01 — Show only the essential current state at a glance.** Complications, widgets, and Live Activities prioritize timely, meaningful information; Always On preserves legibility by quieting secondary detail. Clarte: a small, readable state such as Listening, Composing, Speaking, or Paused can support the main message without adding a dashboard of metrics or tracker telemetry. [Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities).
- **R02 — Preserve spatial landmarks when interaction pauses.** Always On recommends a consistent layout and an unavailable appearance for an inactive control rather than suddenly removing it. Clarte: keep the message and control locations stable during a rest or tracking pause; change the control state without shifting which Q1–Q8 cell owns an action. A dim appearance must remain legible. [Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on).
- **R03 — Update status when something meaningful changes, without continuous decorative motion.** Always On favors infrequent, subtle updates; Live Activities retain the display until underlying content changes. Clarte: update recording, connection, or speech state on meaningful events; avoid pulsing ornaments and raw gaze-coordinate readouts beside the message. This does not justify delaying safety-critical tracking-loss feedback. [Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities).

## Reduce interaction cost without hiding capabilities

- **R04 — Open directly into one relevant task and defer nonessential setup.** App Clips favor a focused flow and immediate usefulness; iMessage extensions protect the conversational flow with one primary experience; Sign in with Apple delays sign-in until it adds value. Clarte: a returning communicator should reach the relevant conversation phase with saved preferences intact; keep optional account or personalization forms out of the path. Necessary permission and tracking-readiness checks still apply. [App Clips](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-clips), [iMessage apps and stickers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/imessage-apps-and-stickers), [Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple).
- **R05 — Give frequent, valuable workflows direct entry points with clear outcomes.** App Shortcuts and Home Screen quick actions expose important tasks without a navigation tour; familiar options reduce recall. Clarte: a saved phrase or the appropriate conversation phase can be reached through a gaze-accessible action instead of repeated menus. Values: Apple's limits are 10 App Shortcuts and 4 Home Screen quick actions; neither changes Clarte's eight-cell action budget. [App Shortcuts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-shortcuts), [Home Screen quick actions](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/home-screen-quick-actions).
- **R06 — Make contextual changes predictable; keep action identity stable.** Quick actions must not change unpredictably; SharePlay ties disorienting role/layout transitions to explicit actions and helps participants understand who caused shared changes. Clarte: do not silently reorder G1–G4 or move Accept/Delete as frequency changes. A suggestion may change within its reserved slot; an explicit phase or page change must make changed action meanings apparent. [Home Screen quick actions](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/home-screen-quick-actions), [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay).

## Tracking, fatigue, and recovery

- **R07 — Show a tracking failure, offer a concrete remedy, and provide an escape from endless recovery.** AR guidance exposes lost face tracking, offers plain-language fixes, and allows relocalization to be cancelled or reset. Clarte: show a short tracking-unavailable state, stop accepting uncertain selections, preserve the draft, and offer a gaze-accessible pause/retry route where feasible. Values: HIG's face-tracking warning example is a loss lasting more than about 0.5 seconds; this is ARKit guidance, not a safe Clarte selection-delay value. [Augmented reality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/augmented-reality).
- **R08 — Design for a comfortable posture and opportunities to rest.** AR guidance recognizes fatigue from sustained device position and recommends downtime rather than demanding large or abrupt movement. Clarte: keep gaze targets stationary, avoid requiring head movement to reach a control, and preserve genuine unowned rest cells or an independently confirmed selection model. The rest-cell mechanism comes from Clarte's contract, not ARKit. [Augmented reality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/augmented-reality).
- **R09 — Keep errors and recovery in the user's available interaction channel.** CarPlay must work while its connected phone is inaccessible and must report problems on the active display. Clarte: a camera, microphone, or decoding failure must not assume the communicator can grab a mouse, type, or use a phone; provide an accessible on-screen recovery path and state honestly when outside help is required. [CarPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carplay).

## Clear language, feedback, and legibility

- **R10 — Name the task or remedy in ordinary, accurate language.** AR and NFC avoid implementation jargon; CareKit favors short familiar names; HealthKit uses the name people recognize rather than the framework name. Clarte: prefer short action labels and concrete fixes such as Turn on a light over internal tracker/decoder terminology. Describe the actual input modality rather than telling a gaze-only user to tap. Do not remove a needed safety or blocked-state explanation merely to shorten it. [Augmented reality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/augmented-reality), [NFC](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/nfc), [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit), [HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit).
- **R11 — Explain failures where they happen with the specific problem and an available fix.** Apple Pay ties errors to the relevant information and distinguishes processing failure from success. Clarte: identify whether microphone permission, tracking, decoding, or speech failed, and give the next feasible action; do not clear the intended message on an error. Values: Apple Pay suggests at most 128 characters for its validation-error detail; treat this as a brevity example, not a universal AAC limit. [Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay).
- **R12 — Verify at-a-distance legibility in the actual environment.** Complications avoid thin strokes; CarPlay tests real-world light; Live Activities prefer large text of medium weight or heavier; widgets simplify distant views and enlarge text. Clarte: inspect action labels, status, and the message from the user's working distance and the partner's position, across lighting and supported appearances. Fewer details are preferable to shrinking critical text. Values: complication strokes generally measure at least 2 pt; visionOS widgets allow 75–125% scaling. These are source-context examples, not Clarte typography or zoom limits. [Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [CarPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carplay), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets).
- **R13 — Preserve meaning without color.** Complications and widgets must communicate in tinted/grayscale mode, and CareKit never relies solely on color for a task's meaning. Clarte: retain A–F/G–N/O–S/T–Z labels and distinct text or shapes for active, pending, and unavailable states; group colors can supplement these signals. [Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit).

## Permission, privacy, and deliberate audio

- **R14 — Ask for the minimum sensitive access at the moment its purpose is clear.** CareKit and HealthKit request data in context, explain why it is needed, and avoid duplicating system permission dialogs. Clarte: request camera access for gaze setup and microphone access when starting partner recording, with short, accurate purpose text; avoid collecting health history just because the app serves a disabled population. Browser permission UI must not be imitated or presented as gaze-accessible if it is not. [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit), [HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit).
- **R15 — Let the communicator decide when audio starts.** CarPlay avoids automatic playback and preserves the person's final volume control; AirPlay streams only expected content. Clarte: keep predictions and drafts silent until the communicator deliberately invokes speech, with the intended message visible for inspection. Navigation, focus, and a candidate becoming available must not start speech. Audio state must distinguish starting, playing, and unavailable. [CarPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carplay), [AirPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/airplay).

## Honest actions and resilient continuity

- **R16 — Make every action-shaped element correspond to a real available capability.** HomeKit omits a microphone button when bidirectional audio is unsupported; Top Shelf avoids implying interactivity in a static image; widgets avoid borrowed appearances with different behavior. Clarte: make read-only transcript/status content visibly different from selectable controls, and avoid dead feature buttons. Distinguish an unsupported capability from a temporarily blocked one whose reason and recovery need to remain discoverable. [HomeKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/homekit), [Top Shelf](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/top-shelf), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets).
- **R17 — Preserve useful work across interruptions without making people manage infrastructure.** iCloud supports resuming app state and unobtrusively indicates when changes cannot reach other devices. Clarte: preserve an in-progress draft when switching phases or handling a recoverable connection problem, and say which feature is unavailable while keeping independent functions usable. Do not promise offline decoding or speech unless implemented; optional sync failure should not block local communication. This does not recommend adding cloud sync. [iCloud](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icloud).
- **R18 — Report the stage actually reached, and end live status when the task ends.** A successful Tap to Pay card read is followed by authorization that can still fail; Wallet distinguishes order receipt, processing, and fulfillment; Live Activities end when their task ends. Clarte: accepting a gaze input, selecting a word, preparing speech, starting playback, and completing playback are different outcomes. Show confirmed state instead of a premature success checkmark; clear Recording or Speaking promptly when it stops. Values: HIG's Live Activity fit is up to 8 hours and its suggested retained summary is 15–30 minutes; neither is an AAC session limit or required summary timer. [Tap to Pay on iPhone](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tap-to-pay-on-iphone), [Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities).
- **R19 — Stop microphone capture when its stated purpose is complete; treat storage as a separate choice.** ShazamKit limits recording to the needed sample and requires opt-in before storing recognized content in a library. Clarte: when the communicator pauses/ends partner-context capture, stop actual microphone capture rather than just changing the icon. Do not silently keep listening during composition; permission to capture does not imply permission to retain or upload audio indefinitely. The exact task boundary must be explicit, not inferred from silence. [ShazamKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shazamkit).
- **R20 — Separate a useful shared status from private content.** Always On, Live Activities, and notifications avoid exposing sensitive content to casual observers; SharePlay allows views suited to participants' different roles. Clarte: the communicator's working draft and recorded context need not appear in a partner-facing or idle view. Show only the agreed shared message or a neutral state, with deliberate control over sensitive disclosure; do not mirror the full composer just because a partner is present. [Always On](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/always-on), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay).
- **R21 — Use one authoritative progress presentation for an operation.** Apple Pay warns that extra spinners duplicate the payment sheet's progress and confuse transaction state. Clarte: show decoding or speech preparation in one relevant place; do not add simultaneous button spinners, banners, overlays, and success toasts for the same work. A dwell-selection indicator serves a separate operation and may still be needed. [Apple Pay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pay).
- **R22 — Keep compact status simple while making pause and stop easy to reach.** Live Activities favor essential direct interactions, preferably a single control; workouts make pause, resume, and stop easy to find and acknowledge. Clarte: a quiet recording/speaking status can offer the immediately relevant stop/pause action, with other tools on the appropriate phase/page. The control still needs its own valid backend ownership; visual compactness never licenses several tiny actions in one cell. Values: Live Activities prefer 1 interactive element; inline accessory widgets have 1 tap target. [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Workouts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/workouts), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets).
- **R23 — Protect costly edits before discarding them, and let people inspect the pending result.** Photo editing confirms cancellation only when changes would be lost and previews edits before approval; iCloud warns when deletion affects every device. Clarte: guard Clear message or leaving a changed draft with an accessible confirmation or recoverable preservation; keep the actual affected message available for inspection. Avoid adding confirmation to every ordinary Delete or to leaving an unchanged/empty draft. Any cross-device deletion must disclose its wider effect. [Photo editing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/photo-editing), [iCloud](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icloud).

## Accessible setup, consent, and attention

- **R24 — Break a demanding setup into short, bounded steps with an explicit finish.** ResearchKit surveys use one question per screen, state expected effort, show progress, and announce completion. Clarte: calibration or access setup can present one current task, truthful progress, and a clear done/retry result, instead of a dense instruction sheet. State an estimated duration only if measured; skipped checks must not look completed. This is a setup-flow analogy, not a recommendation to add surveys. [ResearchKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/researchkit).
- **R25 — Keep consent complete and understandable, including retention and withdrawal.** ResearchKit splits long consent into readable sections while keeping the full form available before agreement; Wallet explains whether data is kept and for how long. Clarte: sensitive recording or research consent must expose what is collected, its use, retention, and how to withdraw where applicable. Progressive disclosure can reduce clutter but must not conceal material terms. Browser camera/microphone permission alone is not consent to an additional research or data-sharing purpose. [ResearchKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/researchkit), [Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet).
- **R26 — Reserve interruption for essential new information; coalesce and do not nag.** Notifications avoid repeats for the same event, CareKit minimizes/coalesces reminders, Live Activities avoid duplicate push alerts, and Wallet stops re-suggesting a declined optional pass addition. Clarte: update foreground state quietly; do not repeat toasts, sounds, or optional setup/telemetry prompts because the communicator has not responded. Keep optional capabilities reachable if they later change their mind, and keep unresolved blocking problems visible rather than repeatedly interrupting. [Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit), [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet).
- **R27 — Make essential information discoverable without hearing a sound or catching a fleeting cue.** Notifications warn against relying on sound, badges, or brief short looks; Siri supports responses that remain understandable in the available modality. Clarte: a tracking-loss, recording, or speech-failure state needs a readable visual representation that remains findable. Optional sounds may supplement it; they must not speak private system status to the partner or replace the visible state. [Notifications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/notifications), [Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri).
- **R28 — Keep each person's comfort and access settings independent.** SharePlay preserves personal volume/subtitle choices instead of changing them for everyone; iCloud syncs only settings people want across devices. Clarte: partner-view preferences must not overwrite the communicator's text size, contrast, or interaction settings. Do not treat a device-specific gaze calibration as universally portable. These are applications of the principle; HIG does not test Clarte's calibration model. [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay), [iCloud](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icloud).
- **R29 — Render essential text as real, scalable text, and let the layout accommodate it.** Widgets avoid rasterized text, support preferred text sizes, and enlarge distant-view text; Wallet does not embed essential information in images. Clarte: use semantic HTML text and accessible names for group labels, messages, and status; check enlargement and localization without cutting off meaning or overlapping another action's cell. Prefer a simpler page to shrinking critical text. Values: widgets generally use at least 11 pt and support Dynamic Type Large–AX5; snippets cap custom views at 400 pt high. These are platform examples, not adequate Clarte font sizes or web layout limits. [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [Wallet](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/wallet), [Snippets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/snippets).
- **R30 — Distinguish current data, stale data, and unavailable measurements.** Widgets show when information was last updated if it may lag; workouts explain what is still measured when a sensor cannot supply a reading. Clarte: never leave an old tracking-ready indication looking current after frames stop, or imply a stale/partial transcript is live. Show a short unavailable/stale state and the remaining usable capability. Do not invent replacement measurements or imply a fallback is equally reliable without evidence. [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets), [Workouts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/workouts).
- **R31 — Use outcome-specific action and clarification labels.** Snippets prefer a specific action verb over OK/Proceed; Siri asks what needs clarification explicitly; App Shortcuts can offer a likely option with a short alternatives list. Clarte: label the release Speak and the destructive action Clear message, rather than ambiguous confirmation words. If a word needs disambiguation, name that word and show a few clear, gaze-addressable alternatives. A likely default is a suggestion, not consent or a committed utterance. [Snippets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/snippets), [Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri), [App Shortcuts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-shortcuts).

## Not applicable

- [Activity rings](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-rings): Move/Exercise/Stand data, Apple-specific ring artwork, and branding rules do not support the current communication task; do not borrow the three-ring motif for dwell or decoder confidence.
- [Apple In-App Purchase](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-in-app-purchase): purchases, subscription offers, billing, Family Sharing, and refunds are outside the scoped AAC flow; no commerce surface is being proposed.
- [Game Center](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-center): achievements, leaderboards, challenges, and multiplayer discovery are unrelated to communication; gamifying speech or gaze performance is not supported by this page.
- [ID Verifier](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/id-verifier): age/identity verification, portraits, and reader requests are outside scope; its data-minimization advice is already represented by the applicable permission/consent sources.
- [Live Photos](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-photos): no Live Photo capture/edit/share task is present; preserving photo frames and using movement to identify them do not add a distinct AAC rule.
- [Maps](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/maps): geographic navigation, pins, zoom-dependent map detail, and place cards are outside scope; generic contrast and hierarchy advice adds no distinct rule beyond R01/R12/R13.
- [Watch faces](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/watch-faces): shareable watch-face configurations, complications, device bezels, and compatibility fallbacks do not add a distinct rule for the scoped browser composer.

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- [App Shortcuts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-shortcuts): voice activation and hardware shortcuts are optional alternatives, not gaze-only access; frequency-based ordering must not move live gaze targets. In-app instructional tips also conflict with minimal-ui's ban on routine explanatory copy.
- [Home Screen quick actions](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/home-screen-quick-actions): touch-and-hold is unavailable to a gaze-only user; transfer direct access and predictability, not the gesture, subtitles, or four-item menu geometry.
- [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit): card stacks, categorical colors, and instruction-heavy task views conflict with minimal-ui if copied literally; use short accurate labels, one task at a time, and color only when it helps find meaning.
- [CarPlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carplay): upper-half placement, touch targeting, and logo-coordinated branding are driving-context advice; Clarte's fixed ownership and purposeful-color rules take precedence.
- [Augmented reality](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/augmented-reality): touch gestures, movement coaching, and near-object hit expansion cannot be imported into noisy quadrant selection; never enlarge one action into a cell owned by another or treat proximity as speech consent.
- [Complications](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/complications): multiple information-rich complications are not a mandate for a Clarte dashboard; tiny watch text and platform slot dimensions are not accessibility sizes for webcam gaze.
- [Live Activities](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/live-activities), [Widgets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/widgets): brand-driven bold color, inset boxes, rotating event displays, surprise treatments, dynamic-height layouts, and animated repositioning conflict with minimal-ui or stable gaze ownership if copied literally; the animation allowance of up to 2 seconds is not an AAC motion recommendation. Simplifying a distant read-only view must not remove the communicator's only reachable stop control.
- [Top Shelf](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/top-shelf): attribution above a title directly conflicts with minimal-ui's nothing-above-titles rule; autoplay, timed scrolling banners, focus-driven animation, and promotional imagery must not be imported as conversation or gaze-selection behavior.
- [ResearchKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/researchkit): long introduction sequences, quizzes, progress dashboards, and comparisons with other participants are not defaults for an AAC app; consent and safety explanations remain permitted by minimal-ui and must not be removed as decorative copy.
- [Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri): spoken activation and open-ended follow-up questions can be expensive or inaccessible with gaze; provide suitable on-screen choices instead. Siri's restrictions on app-authored dialogue do not authorize censoring the communicator's own words.
- [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay), [iCloud](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/icloud): last-change-wins, relying on speech/gestures to negotiate control, and automatic all-content sync cannot establish AAC authorship or privacy; personal access settings and draft control need explicit ownership.
- [HealthKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit), [CareKit](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/carekit): their centralized Health privacy settings do not mean a browser AAC app should remove its own gaze-reachable Pause/Stop controls or pretend it can automate the browser's permission dialog.
- [Workouts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/workouts): automatically discarding an extremely brief workout does not justify discarding a short utterance, draft, or partner recording; duration alone is not evidence of an accidental communication.
