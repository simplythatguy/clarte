# b5-components-layout-platforms

Pages read: **27 of 27**, in full as supplied Markdown. Unread pages: none.

These notes extract the supplied HIG snapshots; they do not claim a fresh check of Apple's live pages or inspection of the original figures/videos. The source-backed rationale precedes each **Clarte:** application. Those applications are proposed transfers for the parent's synthesis, not human-approved product decisions or evidence of webcam-gaze usability. No dwell duration, gaze accuracy, or validated webcam target size is established by this batch.

The current contract remains fixed: 2 rows × 4 columns, Q1–Q8; at most one action per cell; a spanning action owns every cell it touches; only unowned cells are rest zones. Reading space drawn inside an owned cell is not a rest zone. Conditional examples about phrase libraries, settings, or diagnostics do not propose adding those features to the composer.

## Glanceable tasks, orientation, and focus

- **Make the current task understandable in one glance.** watchOS prioritizes concise, targeted interactions on one screen rather than making people inspect a complex interface. **Clarte:** let recording, composing, or reviewing speech be the dominant job of its current view; keep the relevant message and essential action easy to identify, with supporting status quieter. Values: watchOS describes concise interactions lasting **less than a minute** and actions using **one or two gestures**; these describe that platform, not a Clarte completion-time or selection-count promise. [Designing for watchOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos)

- **Use a short, meaningful location label only when it adds information.** Toolbar guidance allows omission of a redundant title and favors a word or short phrase describing the view rather than the app's name. **Clarte:** a concise phase or screen name can orient the user and partner; do not add the app name, a second subtitle explaining the phase, or an eyebrow above it. Values: the HIG title target is **under 15 characters**, not a limit on authored messages or essential clarity. [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)

- **Distinguish the current interaction target from a committed choice.** tvOS makes the focused item apparent; list guidance distinguishes persistent navigation selection from a chosen option marked by a check. **Clarte:** use a stationary, perceptible targeting cue, then a distinct accepted/selected state. A target highlight must not imply that the word was accepted or spoken. Keep dwell progress, if used, visually distinct from the final accepted state; this is a Clarte adaptation, not a tvOS dwell prescription. [Designing for tvOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-tvos), [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)

- **Make relevant content easy to reach without taking over the person's choice.** watchOS recommends timely personalization; tvOS search guidance uses context and recent searches to reduce typing effort. **Clarte:** offer useful phrases or candidate content for the current conversation when such features exist, while leaving acceptance and speech deliberate. Relevant suggestions can shorten input; these sources do not justify automatically committing or speaking them. [Designing for watchOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos), [Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)

## Legibility and stable spatial layout

- **Choose visual density for the viewing distance and input method.** iPadOS explicitly ties content size and density to those conditions; tvOS must remain readable from across a room. **Clarte:** spend available space on readable content and coarse, distinguishable targets rather than importing desktop density merely because the laptop has many pixels. Check the user's and partner's actual viewing positions. Values: HIG describes TV use at **8 feet or more**, iPad use typically within **about 3 feet**, and iPhone use within **a foot or two**. These are platform descriptions, not recommended Clarte seating distances. [Designing for iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ipados), [Designing for tvOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-tvos), [Designing for iOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios)

- **Keep text and actions comfortably perceivable; treat platform dimensions as references, not gaze thresholds.** Game-design guidance emphasizes contrast, readable text, sufficient button size, and separation appropriate to each input method. **Clarte:** allow larger text and generous spacing while keeping every action's actual bounds within the ownership contract. A familiar native button minimum cannot establish a safe webcam-gaze target. Source values, in Apple **points**, are preserved below; they are not CSS-pixel equivalents or proposed Clarte defaults. [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

  | Platform in the source | Typical text / smallest listed text | Typical button / smallest listed button |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | iOS and iPadOS | 17 pt / 11 pt | 44×44 pt / 28×28 pt |
  | macOS | 13 pt / 10 pt | 28×28 pt / 20×20 pt |
  | tvOS | 29 pt / 23 pt | 66×66 pt / 56×56 pt |
  | visionOS | 17 pt / 12 pt | 60×60 pt / 28×28 pt |
  | watchOS | 16 pt / 12 pt | 44×44 pt / 28×28 pt |

  **Source discrepancy:** the same supplied page's prose describes **44×44 pt** as the iOS touch minimum, while its table lists **28×28 pt** as the minimum and **44×44 pt** as the default. Preserve the discrepancy; do not silently pick 28 pt or claim this batch resolved it. Neither figure validates gaze selection.

- **Let people adjust appearance and interaction comfort without making setup a prerequisite for every use.** iOS supports appearance and text-size preferences; macOS and game guidance support personalization, including fonts, motion, and sound. **Clarte:** provide reachable preferences for readable type, theme, motion, and optional feedback; honor relevant existing accessibility preferences and use usable initial settings. Visual customization still has to fit Q1–Q8 and preserve access. [Designing for iOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios), [Designing for macOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-macos), [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

- **Keep choice labels scannable without concealing the message someone is about to authorize.** Lists favor concise rows and preservation of recognizable content when space is tight. **Clarte:** use brief category and control labels, but make the complete candidate or authored utterance readable in genuine rest space before acceptance or speech. Do not transfer middle-ellipsis advice for identifying files to text whose missing words could change the intended meaning. [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)

- **Keep action locations stable while people inspect or operate them.** Collection guidance cautions against unrequested layout changes during interaction; iPhone Duo guidance favors small adjustments over dramatic rearrangement. **Clarte:** avoid moving or reordering actionable predictions under gaze. Changing content or adapting a viewport must not silently change which action the current backend cell owns during a pending selection. This selection guard is a proposed gaze adaptation. [Collections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/collections), [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)

- **Keep visible controls readable and actually reachable around overlays and reserved areas.** Status-bar guidance warns against showing apparently usable controls behind system UI; window guidance prevents window controls from covering toolbar items. **Clarte:** test camera notices, permission UI, browser chrome, safe areas, and open overlays for occlusion of communication and recovery actions. Contrast separation may be needed where content scrolls under controls; decorative blur is not the goal. [Status bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/status-bars), [Windows](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows), [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)

## Navigation and progressive disclosure

- **Keep navigation shallow and expose a small useful set before offering more.** watchOS reduces hierarchy depth; its list guidance recommends short relevant lists with a way to reach the remainder, rather than silently omitting expected items. **Clarte:** show an economical current choice set, with an explicit, gaze-reachable continuation when needed. Preserve the full capability and avoid making every common phrase require a long category drill-down. No fixed list-row count is given. [Designing for watchOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-watchos), [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)

- **Keep destinations and commands semantically distinct.** Tab bars switch between app sections; toolbar controls operate on current content. Tab-view panes should contain related content, with controls affecting their own pane. **Clarte:** do not style Speak, Delete, or Accept as destinations that look equivalent to a library or settings section. Make any mode switch distinguishable from an action that changes or releases the message. [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Tab views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views), [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)

- **Keep navigation availability predictable, including empty states.** The HIG cautions against tabs appearing, disappearing, or becoming unavailable merely because their content is absent. **Clarte:** when a destination exists, keep its location stable and give a short reason or next step if its content is unavailable. This does not require a permanent tab bar on a composer; it applies to navigation when it is presented. [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)

- **Use a few explicit, understandable navigation choices instead of overflowing strips.** Tab guidance warns that overflow makes destinations harder to notice and reach; short labels help people predict the content. **Clarte:** budget every visible tab as an action, alongside typing, recovery, and reading space, and prefer plain labels when an icon could be ambiguous. Values: customizable iPad tab-bar defaults should be **five or fewer**; the tab-view page says to avoid **more than six** tabs and to consider alternatives at **six or more**. These are source-specific complexity guidelines, not permission to place five or six tabs alongside Clarte's four groups. [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Tab views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-views)

- **Show adjacent panes only when they support one coherent task.** Split views can show navigation and its selected content, and should preserve the visible relationship between them; narrow layouts must retain logical navigation. **Clarte:** a small related support area may aid the main task, but a transcript, composer, library, and settings panel should not become equal competing jobs. If a selected list item drives a detail view, keep the selection evident. watchOS demonstrates the alternative of presenting list or detail as a full-screen view. [Split views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views)

- **Restore the place people already reached.** Outline guidance retains expansion choices; tab bars preserve navigation state within their sections. **Clarte:** returning from a relevant secondary view should restore the prior location and disclosed category rather than requiring repeated gaze navigation. Preserve the draft through the transition; draft preservation is also consistent with the cross-display state guidance. [Outline views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/outline-views), [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)

- **Hide advanced details behind a clearly named, reachable disclosure.** Disclosure guidance keeps frequent functionality exposed, labels what will appear, and puts a control near its related content. **Clarte:** keep advanced tracking settings out of the composition surface, with an explicit entry into a view whose actions still obey the cell budget. Values: HIG recommends **no more than one disclosure button per view**; that specific limit concerns the expanding-button component, not every hierarchy triangle or every possible menu. [Disclosure controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/disclosure-controls)

- **Make secondary navigation easy to leave and avoid deep sidebar trees.** Sidebars use substantial space; the HIG supports hiding them and limits their visible hierarchy. **Clarte:** if a phrase library or settings area needs a sidebar, keep it secondary, provide a gaze-operable return or hide control, and recover space for the main content. Do not consume cells with persistent navigation simply because a desktop is wide. Values: HIG generally recommends **no more than two hierarchy levels** in a sidebar. [Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)

## Controls, grouping, and surface restraint

- **Prioritize essential commands and one clear primary action.** Toolbar guidance discourages overcrowding, gives key commands persistent access, and reserves strong emphasis for a single primary action. **Clarte:** make the action appropriate to the current phase visually distinct, keep necessary correction and recovery reachable, and place occasional operations behind the existing feature-page or menu mechanism. Values: HIG suggests **at most three toolbar groups**; grouping does not increase the number of independent actions a backend cell can contain. [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)

- **Group related controls through alignment and spacing before adding containers.** Box guidance warns that nested outlines make an interface busy; toolbar guidance groups by function and frequency while discouraging excessive backgrounds and tinting. **Clarte:** build one coherent composer rather than a card per control, and separate functions with placement and spacing. A semantic group does not require a visible border, a colored section, or another title. [Boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/boxes), [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars)

- **Treat a compound choice as one interaction unit.** A tvOS lockup combines its visual parts into one selectable unit and keeps peer dimensions consistent; collection guidance leaves enough room for feedback to remain distinct. **Clarte:** a phrase, its optional symbol, and its text preview can belong to one action rather than separate tiny targets. Keep peers consistently placed, with feedback contained within their owned cells. Do not import poster-card decoration, expansion, or tilt. [Lockups](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lockups), [Collections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/collections)

- **Make a control's meaning available before it is activated.** Toolbar guidance favors recognizable symbols and clear meanings; iPhone Duo guidance supplies a title even when an item is represented by a symbol. **Clarte:** use familiar icons only, give ambiguous actions a short visible label, and retain accessible names in all representations. A transient hover label cannot be the only way to understand a dwell target. Keep Back and dismissal behavior predictable even if platform-specific placement changes. [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)

## Paging, reading, and search

- **Prefer bounded pages when they make browsing easier to control.** Scroll guidance allows fixed-amount paging with overlap to preserve context; watchOS favors individual pages no taller than one screen; Mac Catalyst explicitly offers Next and Previous alternatives to gestures. **Clarte:** phrase browsing or long transcripts can use stable, large page controls and readable content in unowned cells, rather than requiring a precise scrollbar drag. Values: possible overlap is **a line of text, a row of glyphs, or part of an image**; watchOS suggests **one screen height per page**. These are options, not a mandate to paginate every view. [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views), [Mac Catalyst](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/mac-catalyst)

- **Avoid competing scroll regions that behave alike.** The HIG identifies nested scrolling on the same axis as unpredictable. **Clarte:** prefer one clearly bounded scrolling or paging content region per reading task; do not put a scrollable candidate list inside a scrolling composer on the same axis. Any navigation controls remain distinct, stationary actions under the backend rules. [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)

- **Move content automatically only enough to support the person's current action.** HIG examples reveal the active insertion point, search result, or selection while retaining context. **Clarte:** a deliberate edit can reveal its insertion point, but fresh partner transcript text or predictions should not continually drag a person away from the passage they are reading. Keep action controls anchored; do not treat every update as permission to scroll. [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)

- **Keep eye-driven browsing separate from dense or precise control areas.** visionOS supports Look to Scroll in reading/browsing views, but excludes secondary control-heavy views and removes custom scroll-linked effects. **Clarte:** the transferable principle is to protect control operation from incidental scrolling. Do not claim that looking near a pixel edge can operate a quadrant-only backend; a gaze-scrolling proposal needs an explicit ownership map and reading-safe behavior before adoption. Values: none supplied for a webcam trigger region or dwell. [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)

- **Make the scope of search clear without a paragraph of instructions.** Search guidance uses placement and short placeholder wording to indicate which collection is searched, and generally begins with a broad scope before refinement. **Clarte:** when search exists, keep its relation to the phrase collection or settings apparent, use a short accessible name, and expose filters only when useful. Placeholder-only labeling is appropriate under minimal-ui only when this is the one obvious input. Every clear button or scope control is another action that must fit the active view's cell budget. [Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)

- **Reduce search entry and scanning with relevant suggestions.** Search guidance ranks likely results first; tvOS emphasizes recent and contextual suggestions because typing is costly. **Clarte:** relevant saved phrases or recent queries can reduce group-letter entry, but keep the initial result set manageable and provide access to more. A promising result remains a candidate, not an accepted utterance. [Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields)

- **Make query feedback responsive without stealing the interaction context.** The HIG favors refining results as people type, but warns against unexpectedly covering a search area with a virtual keyboard in some iPad contexts. **Clarte:** query updates can refine results while preserving the group-input surface, the current draft, and stable result targets during selection. Do not autofocus an unrelated text field or let a live re-sort substitute a different phrase under a pending dwell. The selection guard is a gaze adaptation of search responsiveness plus collection stability. [Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields), [Collections](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/collections)

## Platform changes, learning, and recovery

- **Preserve capability and state as the viewport or input context changes.** iPhone Duo guidance retains functionality and state across display changes; Mac Catalyst requires a layout audit rather than assuming automatic scaling produces the right experience. **Clarte:** do not drop Delete, return, pause, or the draft at narrow widths, and do not compress independent targets into the same cell. Recheck the actual Q1–Q8 geometry at every supported size; a camera/display change also needs separate tracking verification, which HIG layout advice cannot supply. Values: Catalyst's iPad idiom scales content to **77%**, including **17 pt → 13 pt** text, while the Mac idiom renders at **100%**. These are native-framework behaviors, not responsive-web scaling instructions. [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo), [Mac Catalyst](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/mac-catalyst)

- **Keep the communication task in one clear active context.** Window guidance discourages unnecessary new windows and distinguishes active input windows from inactive ones. **Clarte:** avoid spawning new windows for ordinary message operations or help; make a temporary secondary task easy to leave. A proposed gaze safeguard is to suspend selection when the communication view is hidden or inactive, rather than allowing background input to mutate a draft. Do not claim the native window model implements this in a browser. [Windows](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/windows)

- **Start with usable defaults and teach operation through a safe action.** Game guidance favors suitable defaults and learning by doing rather than a mandatory written tutorial. **Clarte:** use a short non-speaking practice interaction when teaching a new control or selection mechanism; keep reference help available without permanently adding instructional paragraphs to the composer. This does not justify skipping required calibration or verification, or adding game scoring and animation to communication. [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

- **Request sensitive access when its purpose becomes concrete.** Game guidance introduces sensor/data permissions in the scenario that needs them rather than front-loading unrelated requests. **Clarte:** ask for camera access at tracking setup and microphone access at the relevant recording action, with a short purpose and a usable denied/blocked state. HIG permission timing does not establish a retention policy or substitute for the person's control over partner recording. [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)

## Optional diagnostics only

- **Expose important results without requiring chart manipulation.** Chart guidance makes essential information available without scrubbing and supports accessible summaries and meaningful labels. **Clarte:** if a separate tracking-check or diagnostic view has a chart, show its important measured result in readable text and provide accessible data context; do not require precise gaze on a tiny mark or a tooltip to discover whether the check passed. Keep diagnostics out of the main communication surface unless the current task calls for them. [Charts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charts)

- **Encode measurements honestly and redundantly.** Chart guidance chooses scales for the meaning of the quantity, keeps data more prominent than grid lines, supplements color, and uses actual values rather than subjective descriptions. **Clarte:** label diagnostic values with their quantity and units; use shape or text as well as color; avoid visual decoration or changing scales that imply improvement without a comparable measure. Values: the HIG's bounded-percentage example uses **0–100%**; it is not a basis for inventing a tracking-confidence percentage. [Charts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charts)

## Not applicable

- **Column views:** its native macOS deep-hierarchy browser, resizable columns, and file metadata do not solve the current shallow communication task; adding columns would consume attention and target space without an established need. [Column views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/column-views)
- **Path controls:** file-system ancestry, path menus, and drag-to-select paths have no established role in Clarte's composer; do not add a breadcrumb path just to copy desktop chrome. [Path controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/path-controls)
- **Web views:** this page concerns embedding web content in a native app. Clarte already runs in a web browser, and no embedded-site feature is specified; the native WebKit component and anti-browser-reimplementation advice add no distinct applicable rule here. [Web views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/web-views)

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- **Native size numbers are not gaze access guarantees.** The game page's point sizes describe platform input conventions, and its iOS prose/table minima disagree. Keep the numeric receipt above, flag the discrepancy, and retain the fixed cell contract instead of presenting 28, 44, or 60 pt as a webcam-gaze minimum. [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games)
- **Remote focus expansion is not a safe default for coarse gaze.** tvOS enlarges focused rows and lockups and supports tilt; a direct port could cross owned cell boundaries, displace adjacent controls, or attract gaze. Transfer a clear stationary target state; expansion needs separate geometry and access evidence. [Designing for tvOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-tvos), [Lockups](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lockups), [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables)
- **Pixel-edge eye scrolling needs more than a quadrant label.** visionOS Look to Scroll assumes supported edge regions and a different interaction system. Never treat it as an implementation recipe or evidence that incidental reading can safely drive Clarte scrolling. [Scroll views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/scroll-views)
- **Apple's bars, tables, and disclosures can expose several actions inside one small row.** Search-clear buttons, filter tokens, disclosure accessories, sidebar trees, and tab strips cannot be transplanted densely. Each independent action needs its own cell ownership, including in revealed menus; inapplicable touch/keyboard gestures are not substitute gaze paths. [Search fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/search-fields), [Lists and tables](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/lists-and-tables), [Disclosure controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/disclosure-controls)
- **A large display does not authorize multiple equal jobs.** iPadOS/macOS, Catalyst, and split-view guidance encourage exposing more content or adjacent panes in suitable apps. minimal-ui still permits one dominant job; Clarte must not turn added width into an equally prominent transcript, editor, and dashboard. [Designing for iPadOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ipados), [Designing for macOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-macos), [Mac Catalyst](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/mac-catalyst), [Split views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/split-views)
- **Permanent navigation competes with progressive disclosure.** HIG says a chosen tab bar should remain available during section navigation and advises against hiding a sidebar by default. minimal-ui keeps occasional capabilities out of the main surface. Do not infer that Clarte needs either component; if provided, place it in the relevant navigation context with a reachable return. [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars)
- **Borders, filled selections, materials, accent icons, and cinematic polish are optional platform styling.** Box defaults, tvOS cards, sidebar accent coloring, glass effects, artwork, and audio do not override minimal-ui's neutral surfaces, purposeful color, no item-by-item boxes, and quiet hierarchy. Use ink, weight, or a check for a committed choice, with feedback still perceivable. [Boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/boxes), [Sidebars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sidebars), [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Designing for tvOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-tvos)
- **Hover-only text and gesture-only access are insufficient.** visionOS can reveal labels on look and uses a separate tap; native platforms offer swipes, drags, hover, and shortcuts. Clarte needs understandable labels before selection and explicit gaze-reachable alternatives; reading a revealed label must not itself commit an action. [Toolbars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toolbars), [Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars), [Mac Catalyst](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/mac-catalyst)
- **Instructional copy needs restraint, while meaningful content must remain.** Game tutorials and chart titles/subtitles can support learning and interpretation, but minimal-ui disallows permanent how-to paragraphs and redundant subtitles. Prefer practice and on-demand help; retain concise errors, blocked reasons, accessible descriptions, and useful diagnostic findings. Do not remove the user's message as if it were explanatory UI copy. [Designing for games](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-games), [Charts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/charts)
- **Dynamic device layouts do not change the backend grid.** Duo's vertical bars, fold-aware rearrangement, and new reserved regions are native-device conventions. Transfer state continuity and restrained movement only; a pose, zoom, or resize cannot create extra backend cells or verify that calibration still applies. [Designing for iPhone Duo](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-iphone-duo)
