## Gaze target geometry and visual comfort

- **R01 — Give each gaze action exclusive, generous space.** HIG: small involuntary eye movements make crowded targets hard to isolate; overlapping interactive regions make selection ambiguous. Clarte: one action may own a backend cell or several cells; no second action may use any owned cell. In a dwell interface, reading needs genuinely unowned backend cells. Pixel gutters or a blank visual band inside an owned cell do not prevent activation. Values: Apple Eyes suggests **at least 16 pt around item bounds OR centers at least 60 pt apart**; Spatial layout describes regular controls with **centers at least 60 pt apart and gaps at least 16 pt**. These are visionOS values, not webcam thresholds. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout)

- **R02 — Keep the current task comfortably in view and reduce repeated long eye trips.** HIG: important content belongs within the comfortable field of view, and repeated quick shifts across large areas or different depths can tire the eyes. Clarte: place the draft and the next relevant action within a coherent composition, with quiet secondary controls; check the gaze travel required for composing, reading, accepting, and correcting. A centrally placed action must still own every cell it touches; visual centering cannot bypass the grid. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout)

- **R03 — Use a calm central visual anchor and gently rounded target shapes where a shape is needed.** HIG: sharp corners can attract the eyes away from a target’s center; rounder shapes are easier to aim at. Clarte: center the label or familiar glyph within its owned region, away from neighboring actions. If a button needs a visible shape, prefer restrained rounding. This does not require a card, pill, fill, or border around every group or prediction. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)

- **R04 — Treat an action’s glyph and label as one target.** HIG: a composite interactive component needs one encompassing region that can highlight as a whole. Clarte: looking at either the label or its icon identifies the same action; highlight that action consistently. A spanning component owns all touched quadrants. Do not implement the icon and caption as separate selectable items, and do not confuse a logical hit region with an extra visible wrapper. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)

- **R05 — Keep reading surfaces flat and legible.** HIG: text floating above its background is harder to read; depth on small symbols can reduce legibility, and frequent refocusing can cause fatigue. Clarte: keep the draft, predictions, and letter labels visually stable and flat rather than lifting, extruding, or parallaxing them. Preserve legibility as content is sized for the user. Values: Apple’s **at least 1 m** advice concerns virtual content viewed over time in a headset; it is not a laptop viewing-distance recommendation. [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout), [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)

## Targeting, intention, and feedback

- **R06 — Separate identifying a target from committing its action.** HIG: visionOS uses looking to target and a further gesture to activate; remotes distinguish incidental contact from an intentional press; Pencil hover previews without modifying content. Clarte: entering a quadrant can identify a candidate, but must not immediately accept a word, delete text, or speak. Activation follows the chosen completed dwell or confirmation mechanism. Reading must remain possible without committing; Apple’s eye-plus-hand interaction does not establish a gaze-only confirmation method. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Remotes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/remotes), [Apple Pencil and Scribble](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble)

- **R07 — Make targeting, progress, activation, persistent selection, and unavailability distinguishable.** HIG: focus identifies the interaction target; activation gives immediate feedback; a selected toggle has a persistent state; an unavailable item is visibly inactive. Clarte: a looked-at word must not appear already accepted, and a dwell indicator must not imply that speech has started. Give completed input a brief local acknowledgment and show a toggle’s current value. Keyboard focus and gaze targeting are separate states even if their visual vocabulary is related. [Focus and selection](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection), [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)

- **R08 — Prefer feedback on the owning action to a tiny precision cursor.** HIG: tvOS focuses a finite set of items rather than requiring people to steer a small pointer; if a pointer is necessary it must be easy to see. Clarte: a steady highlight on the Q1–Q8 owner communicates the decision the classifier can actually make. Do not imply pixel accuracy with a crosshair or make the person chase a noisy dot to operate the app. A raw gaze dot can remain a separate diagnostic tool. This is a tvOS-to-quadrant analogy, not a general Apple ban on gaze cursors. [Focus and selection](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection)

- **R09 — Remove movement and decorative detail that compete with the current target.** HIG: peripheral movement and newly revealed nearby content can involuntarily redirect the eyes; dense patterns and high-contrast surroundings distract from a primary task. Clarte: avoid pulsing decorations, animated backgrounds, confetti, flashing indicators, and moving neighboring controls while composing or dwelling. Use contrast to identify the relevant action or problem; a recording state does not need continual motion to remain visible. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Immersive experiences](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/immersive-experiences)

- **R10 — Keep the target’s position and essential content steady during feedback.** HIG: preserving a primary view through a hover transition supplies a stable reference; Pencil previews that fluctuate unnecessarily can distract. Clarte: keep the label, target center, and ownership unchanged while hover or dwell feedback appears. Prefer an in-place change of ink, a modest marker, or progress cue over growth, lifting, or reflow. An expanding tab or pop-out menu beside the fixation is a hazard to evaluate, not a pattern to copy automatically. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Apple Pencil and Scribble](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble)

- **R11 — Delay secondary information according to its purpose, not a guessed dwell threshold.** HIG: subtle hover feedback can appear immediately; expansion can use a short delay; optional extra information such as a tooltip can use a longer delay. Clarte: give targeting feedback without making the person wait for a dramatic reveal, and keep optional help from repeatedly opening during ordinary reading. A help reveal must not change an action’s ownership or become another compulsory step. Values: these pages give **no numeric milliseconds** for the three delay tiers and **no webcam dwell duration**. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)

- **R12 — Do not move focus or replace an action underneath pending input.** HIG: unsolicited focus changes force people to find their place again; writing fields should not move, resize, or autoscroll while they are being used. Clarte: preserve candidate order, visible wording, and target positions during a pending selection. If a candidate or control disappears or changes meaning, do not transfer the accumulated dwell to a different action; cancel or require fresh intent. Do not automatically choose the next prediction merely because the old one vanished. [Focus and selection](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection), [Apple Pencil and Scribble](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble)

- **R13 — Make unavailable input and tracking interruption visible instead of showing stale readiness.** HIG: unavailable gestures need a clear state, and interrupted virtual-hand tracking must not leave an apparently live but frozen representation. Clarte: when gaze input is unusable, stop presenting a stale target/progress indicator as live, suspend its pending activation, and show a short actionable tracking or blocked state. Reacquisition must not unexpectedly complete an old selection. This transfers the interruption principle; it supplies no tracking-loss timeout or confidence cutoff. [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures), [Immersive experiences](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/immersive-experiences)

## Access with little physical effort

- **R14 — Keep the complete core flow usable through the person’s reliable input.** HIG: do not assume a particular gesture or body position; support use with minimal movement; motion-controlled nongame UI can be physically difficult and imprecise. Clarte: for the gaze-only audience, composing, correcting, pausing, and initiating speech need gaze-operable paths. A pinch, head sweep, device tilt, voice command, keyboard press, or partner intervention cannot be the sole way to finish or recover. VisionOS hand gestures do not justify prescribing a blink substitute without checking that user’s access. [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures), [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout), [Gyroscope and accelerometer](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gyro-and-accelerometer)

- **R15 — Support optional alternate inputs without changing the meaning or layout of actions.** HIG: people should be able to move among eyes, keyboard, pointing device, and gestures predictably; keyboard access must reach the interface. Clarte: use accessible web controls with visible keyboard focus, a logical navigation order, and normal activation behavior; preserve the same operations for pointer or partner-assisted use. Optional alternatives broaden access but do not replace the gaze path. Do not import Apple’s framework-specific instruction against custom button keyboard navigation as a reason to omit keyboard access from a web app. [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices), [Keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/keyboards), [Focus and selection](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection)

- **R16 — Keep shortcuts supplemental and essential controls discoverable.** HIG: custom gestures must not be the only route to an important action, and essential pointer-hidden controls need a dependable alternative. Clarte: expose less frequent actions through an explicit gaze-reachable control or menu occupying legal cells. Do not require discovering a hover hotspot, right-click, modifier chord, double-look, or undocumented hold to reach repair or settings. Preserve standard keyboard shortcuts such as undo and cancel rather than assigning them unrelated meanings. [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices), [Keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/keyboards)

- **R17 — Keep cancellation and correction available even in compact views.** HIG: accidental lightweight gestures should avoid destructive effects; compact drawing interfaces may need explicit undo/redo controls; remote navigation preserves a predictable way back. Clarte: retain a reachable way to cancel a pending choice and repair typed content without a fine pointer or a hidden gesture. Opening an auxiliary control must preserve the draft and the item it acts on. Undoing draft edits does not undo speech that a partner has already heard. [Apple Pencil and Scribble](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/apple-pencil-and-scribble), [Remotes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/remotes), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices)

- **R18 — Make mode entry, exit, and return explicit and predictable.** HIG: people should choose when to enter or leave a more immersive context; transitions should be gentle, and an exit control should state whether it returns or quits. Clarte: entering a focused selection or settings view must have a clear gaze-operable return path with a label matching the outcome. Do not rely on browser/system controls to escape. Preserve composition state when returning, and avoid a sudden mode change that remaps actions while the person is still looking at them. [Immersive experiences](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/immersive-experiences), [Designing for visionOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-visionos)

- **R19 — Keep each control’s role simple and its current behavior apparent.** HIG: secondary Action button functions should follow logically from the chosen primary function; too many subsequent meanings increase cognitive load. Clarte: a visible Record/Respond state change may have a clear related role, but do not hide unrelated operations behind dwell length, repeated looks, or several gestures in the same quadrant. One owner per cell is not permission to hide multiple separately intended actions inside that owner. The game advice to combine gestures and watch advice about subsequent presses are not gaze mappings. [Action button](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-button), [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)

## Composition and progressive disclosure

- **R20 — Match the offered keys to the current entry task.** HIG: a custom input view should improve the app’s actual data-entry task, and task-specific keyboards reduce unnecessary work; switching input views should be obvious. Clarte: G1–G4 is a task-specific input model, not a reason to display a standard dense QWERTY layout in eight cells. If number, symbol, or other entry modes exist, make their entry and return reachable through explicit legal targets. Do not present switching to an inaccessible system keyboard as the gaze user’s only fallback. [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards)

- **R21 — Keep the draft and essential actions visible when input controls appear.** HIG: keyboard layout should keep important interface content visible, and accessory controls should relate to the active entry task. Clarte: an input page, candidate list, or menu must not cover the message the person needs to inspect or the controls needed to complete the task. Check quadrant ownership after every layout change; an accessory strip cannot squeeze extra actions into already-owned cells. The separate movable visionOS keyboard does not remove layout obligations in a browser. [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards)

- **R22 — Show one relevant set of controls and make unavailable states honest.** HIG: camera overlays should avoid duplicating controls; game controls can reflect the current context; camera controls can be disabled when their mode is inapplicable. Clarte: avoid parallel button bars for the same operation, and reveal only controls relevant to the current task. When removing an unavailable action would shuffle familiar targets, keep its place with a clear inactive state instead. Contextual simplification must not remove access to capabilities or silently change cell ownership. [Camera Control](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/camera-control), [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)

- **R23 — Give frequent controls a consistent, uncluttered home.** HIG: ornaments keep often-needed controls in a predictable location near their content; too many ornaments add visual weight, and borderless buttons may be sufficient. Clarte: gather the current task’s necessary controls into one coherent region with stable legal owners. Keep rarely used settings one step away rather than giving every function a permanent floating panel or card. Use actual backend rest cells for reading rather than counting visual air around the control region as safe. [Ornaments](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ornaments)

- **R24 — Label actions by their effect with short, familiar words or symbols.** HIG: action labels should be succinct; game controls should represent what they do rather than use abstract controller letters. Clarte: use names such as “Speak,” “Accept word,” or “Delete group,” rather than internal identifiers such as AC, D, or R1/R2. Use a familiar icon only when its action is clear, with an accessible name and a short visible label when ambiguity remains. Values: Action button shortcut labels have a **maximum of three words**, start with a present-tense verb, and omit filler; this is a useful style model, not a universal three-word cap on all app text. [Action button](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-button), [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)

- **R25 — Show understandable current values and make common choices easy to land on.** HIG: Camera Control values need meaningful units and prominent common values; Digital Crown changes need visible feedback and a rate that preserves selectability. Clarte: for a gaze-operated setting, expose readable values in the control and favor a bounded picker, preset, or increment action over requiring precise dragging. Each adjustment action still needs an exclusive owner. Do not turn coarse gaze motion into a rapidly moving slider or infer a recommended dwell duration from this hardware guidance. [Camera Control](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/camera-control), [Digital Crown](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/digital-crown)

- **R26 — Put optional learning outside the live keyboard and avoid instructional pointer clutter.** HIG: a novel keyboard may benefit from help in its app, but help should not fill the keyboard itself; pointer-attached instructions make an interface look complicated. Clarte: if learning support is needed, offer an optional, focused practice experience rather than permanent explanation paragraphs beside G1–G4, repeated subtitles, or instructions following the gaze indicator. Essential labels, current values, and short recovery errors remain visible. The suggestion to add tutorials must be reconciled with minimal-ui, not treated as permission for onboarding copy everywhere. [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices)

## Optional sensory feedback and verification

- **R27 — Make sound and haptics optional supplements to visible feedback.** HIG: haptics complement visual or audible feedback and must remain optional; custom keyboard clicks follow a system preference. Clarte: a person must be able to understand targeting, acceptance, recording, and errors with audio muted and no haptic device. Do not require hands on a trackpad/controller to feel success. Keep the visible state and accessible status meaningful. System keyboard-click guidance is not a browser requirement to emit sound on every group selection. [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics), [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards)

- **R28 — Keep optional event cues brief, local, and causally timed.** HIG: frequent haptics become tiring; short cues usually suit discrete app events; sensory feedback should align with the event it represents. Clarte: if an acceptance cue is enabled, pair it with the completed input rather than every noisy change of gaze quadrant, and avoid continuous vibration or a stream of hover sounds. For future hardware feedback, check that vibration does not disturb camera or microphone use; Apple explicitly identifies these possible interactions. [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)

- **R29 — Give distinct outcomes consistent signals.** HIG: reusing the same haptic for failure and success breaks learned cause and effect; standard patterns have specific meanings. Clarte: keep target acquisition, accepted input, recording start/stop, completion, and failure distinguishable in the feedback vocabulary that is actually offered. A targeting cue must not masquerade as acceptance or successful speech output. Use the same cue for the same outcome across modes, without adding a cue for every possible event. [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)

- **R30 — Validate the interaction on its actual input hardware and realistic tasks.** HIG: hover effects require testing on the device where they are experienced; custom gestures need real-use testing; feedback balance needs user testing. Clarte: test the proposed adaptations with the webcam classifier and intended access conditions, including jitter, drift, fatigue, reading without activation, prediction updates, and loss/reacquisition of tracking. Check whether people can recover from errors without leaving gaze input. A Vision Pro analogy, saved HIG text, or pointer simulation does not establish webcam accuracy, comfort, or a safe dwell threshold. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures), [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics)

## Platform values that must not become webcam defaults

| Source value | What it describes | Transfer limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| At least 16 pt margin, or at least 60 pt between centers | Eye-target separation in [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes) | Not a statement that a target must be 60×60 CSS px. Preserve the generous-separation principle and the fixed cell ownership. |
| At least 60 pt between centers, with at least 16 pt gaps | Regular visionOS controls in [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout) | The page defines visionOS points by visual angle, unlike 2D display points. No direct CSS-pixel conversion or webcam accuracy claim follows. |
| At least 1 m | Comfortable distance for sustained viewing of virtual content in [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes) | Not a required webcam/laptop viewing distance. |
| About 12 pt extra padding with a bezel; about 24 pt without one | iPadOS pointer hit regions in [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices) | Relevant to the distinction between visible shape and hit region, not permission to overlap cell owners or create pixel-based rest zones. |
| 44×44 pt frequent controls; 28×28 pt less important controls | Touch game controls in [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls) | Finger-oriented values, not coarse webcam minima; do not shrink gaze repair/pause controls to the secondary touch size. |
| At most three words | Action shortcut names in [Action button](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-button) | A transferable label-writing model, not a universal limit on content, errors, or accessible names. |
| Immediate / short delay / longer delay | Custom hover timing tiers in [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes) | No numeric durations are supplied; hover timing is not a dwell-selection policy. |

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- **Eye-plus-hand activation is not gaze-only access.** Copying tap/pinch or substituting an assumed blink would leave an unevidenced access path. Keep the selected gaze completion mechanism explicit; an eight-action dwell layout has no reading rest cell. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes), [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures)
- **Pixel spacing is not backend separation.** Apple’s 16/60 pt guidance, pointer padding, and touch sizes cannot override one owner per Q1–Q8 cell or create rest inside an owned cell; the angular-point distinction also prevents a literal CSS translation. [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices), [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **Containing shapes are not a license for a box per item.** Apple’s compound-target region and rounded-shape guidance must coexist with minimal-ui’s unified surfaces and prohibition on pills/cards around every option. Define shared target ownership and a clear visual anchor; add a visible button shape only when necessary. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)
- **Lift, parallax, scaling, and automatic expansion can undermine a stationary gaze target.** Preserve local feedback without moving the label, enlarging into another owner, decorating each item, or exposing new nearby content during a dwell. [Focus and selection](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/focus-and-selection), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices), [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)
- **Quiet secondary controls cannot become tiny or inaccessible to gaze.** Minimal-ui’s size hierarchy and edge placement must respect the backend action budget: less prominent ink may be appropriate, but repair, pause, and settings still need a usable legal target. Apple’s 28 pt secondary touch control is not an exception. [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls), [Spatial layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/spatial-layout)
- **Always-visible ornaments do not justify permanently exposing rarely used settings.** Retain stable access to what is necessary now; move occasional functions behind one explicit control instead of adding several floating toolbars. Borderless controls support the owner’s preference for fewer surfaces. [Ornaments](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/ornaments)
- **Keyboard tutorials and usage tips conflict with minimal-ui when placed in the working screen.** Keep optional learning separate from the live composer, with no instructional paragraphs, repeated subtitles, or content above the title. Short labels, errors, blocked reasons, and necessary recovery information remain allowed. [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards), [Pointing devices](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pointing-devices)
- **Gesture multiplexing and long/repeated physical inputs are not extra gaze actions.** Game advice to combine actions using double taps/holds and watch advice for secondary presses cannot pack several intended actions into one fixed owner or impose repeated body motions. The watch preference against using a subsequent Action button press to stop an activity also does not prescribe AAC stop-speech behavior. [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls), [Action button](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-button), [Gestures](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gestures)
- **Keyboard click or tactile feedback cannot be assumed.** The HIG suggestion to play system keyboard sounds and tactile game press feedback must be reconciled with feedback that people can mute and gaze users who are not touching hardware. Do not make recording or communication depend on either cue. [Virtual keyboards](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/virtual-keyboards), [Playing haptics](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-haptics), [Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
- **visionOS gaze privacy is platform-managed, not inherited by a webcam web app.** The statement that apps do not receive raw look data before a tap describes Apple’s mediation. It does not establish Clarte’s camera permission, processing, storage, or privacy behavior. No such implementation was assessed here. [Eyes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/eyes)
