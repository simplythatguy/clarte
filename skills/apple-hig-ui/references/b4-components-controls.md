## Scope and evidence

These are paraphrased findings from the supplied Apple HIG Markdown snapshots, not a live refresh of Apple's website. Text was read; images missing from the Markdown were not reconstructed or visually inspected. The **Clarte:** clauses are proposed applications for the parent to synthesize, not Apple requirements, accepted product choices, or evidence of webcam accuracy, reduced fatigue, or clinical benefit. No implementation or participant testing was performed.

The existing Clarte contract controls every adaptation: fixed 2-row × 4-column Q1–Q8 classification, at most one action per backend quadrant, a spanning action owning every quadrant it touches, and genuinely unowned quadrants for rest. A visually empty strip inside an owned quadrant is not a rest zone. Menus, fields, secondary controls, and temporary views must obey the same contract. HIG point sizes and platform input conventions do not establish adequate webcam-gaze targets. These pages give no webcam dwell duration or numerical contrast ratio.

## Hit targets, button purpose, and hierarchy

- **Provide a generous hit region and separation around every action.** HIG treats size and surrounding space as necessary for distinguishing and activating buttons through different input methods. **Clarte:** use the inherited quadrant ownership model, including for menus and secondary controls; do not pack several nominally large buttons into one cell. **Values:** HIG button minimum 44 × 44 pt; visionOS 60 × 60 pt, centers at least 60 pt apart, plus 4 pt padding around buttons measuring 60 pt or larger; macOS image buttons approximately 10 pixels between image and button edges. These are platform values, not CSS-pixel conversions or sufficient webcam-gaze targets. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)

- **Prefer calm, rounded visual anchors when a button needs an outline.** The visionOS guidance explains that corners attract the eye away from a shape's center and favors circles or capsules. **Clarte:** a rounded, centered anchor is a candidate treatment within the much larger backend hit region; test it rather than claiming the headset finding proves webcam benefit. Keep the surrounding composition neutral and avoid adding a filled surface to every action. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)

- **Show visible feedback for activation and reflect the actual resulting state.** A custom button needs a press state; a control needs updates during an action and after completion. **Clarte:** distinguish looking, dwell progress, committed selection, and ongoing work so a gaze highlight is not mistaken for an accepted word or completed recording action. Do not leave a stale active state after failure. The particular gaze state machine is an adaptation, not specified by these pages. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls)

- **Give the current task one prominent, nondestructive primary action.** HIG warns that too many prominent buttons increase decision effort and that a destructive button must not receive the primary role. **Clarte:** use one primary action under minimal-ui; keep Clear or Discard out of the default action role. A mode change must make the new action's meaning clear. **Values:** HIG generally allows one or two prominent buttons per view; the owner's rule narrows this to one. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)

- **Use a familiar icon or a short action label that predicts the result.** HIG favors recognizable symbols and concise verb phrases when text is clearer. **Clarte:** prefer labels such as Record, Pause, Speak, or Delete over unexplained abbreviations or ambiguous custom glyphs. Do not make a tooltip the only explanation available through gaze. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus)

- **Keep comparable choices visually consistent.** HIG recommends equal-sized peer buttons and equal-width segments, using visual style to identify a preferred choice within a set. **Clarte:** keep G1–G4 or candidate-choice peers consistent rather than changing a target's size when it becomes likely; reserve size hierarchy for different levels of importance, subject to quadrant ownership. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls)

## States and grouped choices

- **Use toggles for opposing states, and distinguish them from commands.** HIG defines a toggle as managing a two-state setting, rather than choosing from a list or performing an unrelated action. **Clarte:** an on/off setting can be a toggle; selecting a letter group or sending speech is a command. Do not present a single ambiguous control as both a setting and an unrelated action. [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles)

- **Make selected and unselected states distinguishable without color alone.** HIG supports checkmarks, dots, and changed inner details as state cues. **Clarte:** use a checkmark, clear text/value, or another noncolor cue for selected settings and recording state; keep color purposeful rather than making every state a new hue. A multi-setting control must not imply that all settings are on when their state is mixed. [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles)

- **Group only closely related choices and keep their behavior consistent.** HIG cautions against mixing selection-state segments with momentary action segments, or mixing text-only and image-only segments in one group. **Clarte:** use a coherent group for alternatives such as voice choices; do not put a setting choice, Delete, and Speak into one segmented selector. Use short nouns for alternatives and action verbs for commands. A group that already explains itself needs no extra introductory sentence. [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls)

- **Keep choice groups small enough to understand at a glance.** HIG limits long segment and radio-button sets because they take space and navigation effort. **Clarte:** the inherited backend budget and reading/rest needs may require fewer choices than Apple's limits; do not interpret those limits as permission to add compact controls. **Values:** roughly 5–7 segments on a wide interface, no more than about 5 on iPhone; radio-button sets typically 2–5, with another chooser considered above about 5. [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls), [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles)

## Choosing values and entering text

- **Make a chooser predictable before opening it.** HIG recommends logically ordered picker values, meaningful defaults, and a pop-up button that communicates its selection or purpose. **Clarte:** show a voice or timing setting's current value in its control and keep the option order understandable; keep enough task context to explain what will change. Reuse accepted user values rather than replacing them with an invented default. **Values:** the date-picker example reduces 60 minute choices to quarter-hour values 0, 15, 30, 45; this illustrates choosing useful granularity, not a Clarte timing recommendation. [Pickers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pickers), [Pop-up buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pop-up-buttons), [Combo boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/combo-boxes)

- **Offer likely choices while preserving a route to a custom value.** HIG's combo box combines suggestions with custom entry, and pop-up menus can place occasional custom choices behind one option. **Clarte:** predictions or phrase lists must not be treated as the entire space of what a person can express; keep the existing composition/custom-entry route available when all suggestions are wrong. Do not infer that Apple validates Clarte's decoder or spelling fallback. [Combo boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/combo-boxes), [Pop-up buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pop-up-buttons)

- **Show the value being adjusted and provide an efficient way to make larger changes.** HIG recommends exact readouts, optional direct entry, steppers for small increments, and live feedback. **Clarte:** a settings adjustment should show its current value and units; quadrant-sized decrease/increase actions or a small set of presets are candidates for gaze access, rather than a tiny draggable thumb or dozens of repeated dwells. Keep horizontal minimum on the leading side and maximum on the trailing side; vertical minimum belongs below maximum. **Values:** macOS offers a 10-times-increment Shift-click example, but that shortcut is not a gaze solution. [Sliders](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sliders), [Steppers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/steppers)

- **Use a readable multiline region for a message, with type that survives enlargement.** HIG separates small single-line inputs, static labels, and long or editable text views, and calls for legibility with text-size and bold-text accessibility options. **Clarte:** let composed speech wrap naturally and remain readable at browser zoom and larger text settings; do not force a complete reply into a short field or style it as a dense set of controls. [Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields), [Text views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-views), [Labels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/labels)

- **Keep a field's purpose identifiable after entry starts.** HIG notes that placeholders disappear and recommends clear label associations, consistent spacing, and logical focus order. **Clarte:** retain concise labels in multi-field settings; use a lone placeholder only for an otherwise obvious single input, as minimal-ui permits. Do not add helper prose that merely repeats the label. Keep auxiliary keyboard navigation logical without depending on a keyboard for the gaze path. [Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields)

- **Validate at an appropriate boundary and respect local number formats.** HIG ties validation timing to the task and warns that numeric presentation varies by locale. **Clarte:** validate a committed settings value and show a concise corrective error; do not reject an incomplete G1–G4 sequence merely because it is not yet a word. Pending group input is an adaptation, not an HIG validation example. [Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields)

- **Keep the full value readable without requiring hover.** HIG warns about truncated combo-box choices and provides wrapping, truncation, and expansion options for text fields. **Clarte:** preserve enough space for the person's message and candidate wording; if content must be shortened, provide a deliberate gaze-accessible way to inspect it. A hover tooltip is supplementary and cannot make clipped meaning safe to accept or speak. [Combo boxes](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/combo-boxes), [Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields)

- **Time suggestion updates so they do not distract from composition.** HIG explicitly allows delaying suggested tokens because immediate suggestions can distract typing. **Clarte:** treat update timing and stability during reading or dwell as design questions; do not let a visually changing suggestion silently substitute a different intended acceptance. **Values:** this page specifies no delay in milliseconds and establishes no safe dwell/update policy. [Token fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/token-fields)

- **Let people copy useful content without turning every piece of text into a target.** HIG supports copying useful static text and distinguishes content from control labels. **Clarte:** offer an explicit Copy route for a reply or a useful error, with its own valid quadrant when shown; preserve ordinary browser selection for users who can use it. Do not make each word independently actionable inside one backend quadrant. [Text views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-views), [Labels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/labels), [Edit menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/edit-menus)

- **Use an actual button when an image performs an action.** HIG distinguishes passive image display from image buttons and warns that text over images can become illegible. **Clarte:** a pictorial phrase or action needs a named button and a valid gaze action region; keep display-only illustrations passive. Avoid adding image backgrounds beneath wording that the person must read precisely. [Image views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/image-views)

## Progress, status, and interruption control

- **Report measurable progress honestly, and use indeterminate feedback when the remaining work is unknown.** HIG prefers determinate indicators when duration can be estimated, warns about misleading advancement, and asks for actionable feedback when work stalls. **Clarte:** measured dwell progress may show progress toward its configured threshold; decoder/provider completion must not receive a made-up percentage. A stalled task needs a visible problem and recovery route. **Values:** HIG's cautionary example is 90% in 5 seconds followed by the last 10% taking 5 minutes, not a permitted pacing target. [Progress indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators)

- **Keep progress feedback local and geometrically stable.** HIG recommends a consistent location, small inline activity indicators, and avoiding a disruptive spinner-to-bar shape change. Indicators are temporary. **Clarte:** show relevant work at the initiating action or a consistent quiet status position; avoid shifting gaze targets as status changes, and remove the indicator after completion. Add a short status only when the existing context is insufficient. [Progress indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators), [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)

- **Provide a usable stop, pause, or cancel route for interruptible work.** HIG recommends cancellation when safe and communicating any loss of progress. **Clarte:** processing must not trap the person in an unresponsive busy view; a shown escape action must be reachable by gaze and preserve composed text when the operation permits it. Explain a real loss consequence concisely before it happens. Do not promise pause/resume if the provider cannot support it. [Progress indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators)

- **Use a gauge only for a meaningful range, with a readable current value and endpoints.** HIG calls for succinct value/end labels, including for VoiceOver, and favors a continuous capacity style for large ranges. **Clarte:** if a measured level or tuning value is useful, expose its meaning accessibly; do not invent a reassuring tracking-confidence gauge from an unvalidated number. Use color only for meaningful thresholds and never as the sole cue. [Gauges](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gauges)

## Pages and menus

- **Keep feature pages purposeful and show position without creating tiny gaze targets.** HIG page controls describe flat, ordered peers; visionOS uses them as passive indicators, and watchOS favors distinct, glanceable pages. **Clarte:** a quiet current-page indication can accompany the existing feature-page action, while the actual page-switch control keeps valid quadrant ownership. Preserve a clear task on each page and usable text at enlargement. **Values:** more than about 10 dots are hard to count; avoid more than 2 indicator-image types; watchOS suggests a single screen height where useful. None of these requires adding dots, shrinking text, or changing Clarte's approved two-page feature structure. [Page controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/page-controls)

- **Keep primary actions discoverable and put occasional related commands one step away.** HIG warns against hiding all actions in one pull-down menu and explains the discoverability cost of an unspecified More menu. **Clarte:** the current communication action stays visible; secondary options can use a clearly named entry, with a menu that obeys the backend grid. **Values:** HIG suggests at least 3 items can make opening a pull-down worthwhile; it suggests direct buttons/toggles for only 1–2. Do not add filler commands merely to reach that count. [Pull-down buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pull-down-buttons)

- **Make every essential command available through the app's usable input path.** HIG treats context menus, Dock menus, dynamic items, and hidden menu bars as supplementary routes rather than sole access. **Clarte:** provide an explicit, gaze-reachable entry; long press, secondary click, hover, modifier keys, and operating-system menus cannot be the only route to repair, pause, settings, or speech control. Alternate pointer/keyboard shortcuts may remain supplementary. [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus), [The menu bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar), [Dock menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dock-menus)

- **Keep menus short, logically grouped, and shallow.** HIG orders important commands early and keeps related commands together so people can find them without reading every item. **Clarte:** use a small, stable set of relevant commands with space or alignment for grouping; every choice still needs its own quadrant, with reading/rest accommodated. Avoid a moving hierarchy that requires precise pointer travel. **Values:** generally no more than 1 submenu level; reconsider a submenu above about 5 items; at most about 3 groups in a context menu. A repeated term in more than 2 commands is an HIG prompt to consider grouping, not an obligation to add a submenu. [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus), [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus)

- **Handle unavailable commands deliberately rather than mixing menu conventions.** HIG keeps unavailable main/menu-bar items visible but disabled for discovery, while context menus generally omit irrelevant items. **Clarte:** main-menu positions can remain stable while unavailable commands are inert; contextual filtering must not silently move an action into the quadrant the person is already selecting. A concise blocked reason may be necessary under minimal-ui. This selection-safety condition is Clarte-specific. [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus), [The menu bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar), [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus)

- **Distinguish an action's name from a setting's current state.** HIG warns that bare on/off labels can be ambiguous and supports explicit verbs or checkmarks. **Clarte:** make it clear whether a recording control reports the current state or initiates the next action; a highlighted Record label alone should not have to carry both meanings. In settings, show the selected value in its control. [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus), [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles), [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls)

- **Support recoverable editing and name what an edit will affect.** HIG recommends undo/redo, distinguishing Cut from Delete, and context-specific Undo/Redo titles. **Clarte:** an accessible recovery route should make the affected group, word, or operation understandable; a whole-draft clear must not masquerade as deleting one group. Preserve native editing for users who can use it, while providing required gaze controls without duplicating visible actions unnecessarily. This does not change the approved Delete behavior or claim that Undo is already implemented. [Edit menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/edit-menus), [The menu bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar)

- **Keep the target and effect of a contextual command visible.** HIG recommends placing visionOS menus near their affected content and moving edit menus when they would cover important information. **Clarte:** a correction chooser should identify the affected word or message without obscuring the wording needed for the choice; visual proximity remains subordinate to valid quadrant ownership. Do not claim that a near-caret menu can be selected precisely through the backend. [Menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/menus), [Edit menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/edit-menus), [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus)

- **Use different chooser behavior for a selected value and an immediate command.** HIG pop-up buttons choose one mutually exclusive state and can show the selected item, while pull-down buttons execute related commands and can support other menu structures. **Clarte:** a voice selector should display the chosen voice; an editing menu should describe commands. Avoid an ambiguous generic drop-down for both. The open choices still require a gaze-compatible presentation rather than native-sized rows. [Pop-up buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pop-up-buttons), [Pull-down buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pull-down-buttons)

- **If sharing is provided, offer relevant actions once and label their purpose clearly.** HIG discourages duplicate standard actions, brand names in command titles, and irrelevant activities. **Clarte:** an optional reply-sharing route should use familiar wording and preserve the person's deliberate choice of what is shared; it is not the same action as speaking aloud. A native share sheet is not assumed gaze-accessible. **Values:** HIG suggests about 70 × 70 pixels for a custom activity icon's artwork area; that is not a hit target. [Activity views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-views)

## Alerts, sheets, and temporary supporting views

- **Use a choice view for an intentional action and an alert for a critical interruption.** HIG distinguishes an action sheet that clarifies an initiated action from an alert about a serious problem or consequence. **Clarte:** optional phrase/edit choices should not look like errors; reserve an interrupting alert for information that requires immediate attention or an important irreversible decision. Keep the choices within the actual gaze budget. [Action sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets), [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)

- **Avoid modal confirmation for routine, undoable actions.** HIG discourages informational/startup alerts and repeated warnings for common recoverable deletion. **Clarte:** do not ask for another confirmation after every typed group or ordinary Delete; provide a repair route. Show a recoverable connection problem or ordinary status in context; do not hide a problem to make the screen minimal. Confirmation for an irreversible operation remains a separate question. [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)

- **Make a necessary interruption short, specific, and actionable.** HIG favors neutral descriptions, useful result labels, and no explanation of already-clear buttons. **Clarte:** describe the actual failure or loss, then offer meaningful short actions; avoid generic Error, unexplained codes, or Yes/No decisions. **Values:** alerts have up to 3 buttons; action-sheet titles should fit 1 line where possible, alert titles about 2 lines or fewer, and alert button labels ideally 1–2 words. watchOS action sheets allow no more than 4 buttons including Cancel, leaving at most 3 other choices. These are ceilings/examples, not a requirement to use every slot. [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts), [Action sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets)

- **Make destructive consequences clear and preserve an explicit safe exit.** HIG flags destructive commands and provides Cancel for rejecting them; placement conventions vary across menu, action-sheet, and alert contexts. **Clarte:** label a whole-draft destructive choice specifically and provide a valid Cancel target. Do not transfer Apple's platform-specific default-button or placement exceptions into an automatic dwell acceptance. Keep Cancel visually and spatially unambiguous without inventing a universal destructive-button order. [Pull-down buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pull-down-buttons), [Action sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets), [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)

- **Present one scoped temporary task at a time.** HIG rejects stacked sheets and cascading popovers and suggests alternatives for complex or prolonged tasks. **Clarte:** a settings or repair flow should have one clear active context; suspended parent actions must not compete for the same gaze quadrants. A longer task may warrant its own screen rather than a growing stack of overlays. **Values:** one sheet or popover at a time; do not create a hierarchy of popovers. Alerts are an HIG exception above a popover, not encouragement to nest flows. [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets), [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers), [Activity views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-views)

- **Give Done, Cancel, and Back distinct meanings and an accessible exit.** HIG uses Done to finish/save, Cancel or Close to dismiss without saving, and Back to move to a prior step; Done must have an alternative. **Clarte:** do not force someone to save an unwanted setting merely to leave. A gaze-reachable dismissal action is required even when a platform also supports swipe or outside-click dismissal. **Values:** avoid showing all 3 of Cancel, Done, and Back together. [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)

- **Preserve work when a temporary view disappears incidentally.** HIG says an automatically closed nonmodal popover saves work and discards it only through explicit Cancel; sheet dismissal must account for unsaved changes. **Clarte:** retain the in-progress draft or setting edit according to the chosen commit model rather than silently losing it on incidental dismissal. Saving an edit must not automatically speak/share it, and this finding does not choose persistent storage or transcript retention. [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers), [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets)

- **Keep temporary supporting content small and anchored to its task without obscuring essentials.** HIG popovers serve a few related tasks, avoid covering their source or essential content, and give way to full-screen presentations in compact layouts. **Clarte:** preserve the message and the chooser's purpose, but use a full task view when the available quadrants cannot support a small overlay. Do not use a transient popover for a warning that must be noticed. [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers)

- **Keep supporting settings subordinate to the communication task.** HIG panels should provide relevant, direct adjustments, appear only when useful, and avoid competing with content; a decorative HUD can confuse. **Clarte:** keep tuning out of the main composition flow until requested, then present it as the one active task with coarse accessible adjustments. Avoid an always-open inspector or a second equally prominent pane. This adapts the useful principle without importing macOS floating windows. [Panels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/panels)

- **Name supporting UI by what the person does there.** HIG recommends task/panel titles instead of interface terms such as popover or panel. **Clarte:** use Voice or Adjust timing where appropriate, without exposing component names or adding instructions explaining their mechanics. [Panels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/panels), [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers)

- **Keep lengthy-operation status reachable after a temporary action view closes.** HIG activity views dismiss before some background work completes and recommend checking progress in the main app, without a notification for routine completion. **Clarte:** if a later export/share task continues, leave an honest, quiet status and recovery route available; do not imply success from dismissing its chooser. This is conditional guidance, not a request to add sharing. [Activity views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-views), [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls)

## Not applicable

- [Rating indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/rating-indicators): no ranking/rating task is described for this communication flow; star layout and inline rating changes add no necessary Clarte rule.
- [Image wells](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/image-wells): editable-image drag/drop and default-image recovery are outside the stated flow; do not add image editing to use this guidance.
- [Color wells](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color-wells): arbitrary color editing is outside the task; native color wells and drag/drop do not solve gaze-accessible appearance settings.
- [Digit entry views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/digit-entry-views): the tvOS PIN-entry component is outside the browser composition flow; no PIN task was requested. Its secure-entry advice does not justify adding one.

## Conflicts with minimal-ui or with Clarte's gaze model

- **Prominent-action count:** HIG permits 1–2 prominent buttons; minimal-ui requires one. Retain one primary action for a Clarte screen. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons)
- **Filled button surfaces and selected fills:** visionOS generally favors visible button shapes/fills, and toggle examples use filled backgrounds; minimal-ui rejects a fill or card for every peer item and filled boxes to mark selected list items. Keep clear affordances using grouping, placement, ink, or checks, with a prominent surface only where the owner's rules allow it. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles)
- **Peer equality versus importance hierarchy:** HIG discourages different button sizes within one peer-choice set; minimal-ui uses size for importance. These can coexist by keeping peer alternatives equal while sizing different levels of the screen according to importance; neither permits invalid quadrant ownership. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls)
- **Extra explanation and invisible icon meaning:** HIG sometimes suggests introductory text, optional explanations under a chooser, or unlabeled icons; minimal-ui rejects redundant explanation and requires a word for an unfamiliar icon. Use necessary field labels, short unambiguous actions, errors, blocked reasons, and boundaries; do not make gaze users wait for a tooltip to understand an action. [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls), [Pop-up buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pop-up-buttons), [Toggles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/toggles)
- **Color gradients, branding, and animated symbols:** gauge gradients and branded/animated system controls are optional HIG treatments, while minimal-ui allows color only for finding something important; animation may also distract gaze. Keep only a justified semantic cue, with a noncolor equivalent, and assess motion separately. [Gauges](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/gauges), [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls)
- **Native sizes and control density:** 44/60-pt hit regions, 60-pt center spacing, 5–7 segments, compact rows, small Clear accessories, and adjacent stepper halves cannot override one action per fixed quadrant. No arithmetic conversion makes them demonstrated webcam targets. [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls), [Text fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/text-fields), [Steppers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/steppers)
- **Focus or gaze is not activation:** tvOS segments can change selection on focus, while visionOS assumes its own look-and-gesture input. Clarte cannot directly copy focus-triggered selection without risking choices during reading; dwell or deliberate confirmation must retain the existing separation. Passive page-position indicators can transfer; focus-triggered state changes cannot be assumed safe. [Segmented controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/segmented-controls), [Page controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/page-controls)
- **Gesture and precise-pointer assumptions:** slider dragging, wheel scrolling, scrubbing dots, caret selection, token dragging, long press, secondary click, and modifier-key commands are not available merely because a webcam returns Q1–Q8. Keep deliberate coarse actions reachable, and do not equate long dwell with a native long press. [Sliders](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sliders), [Pickers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pickers), [Page controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/page-controls), [Token fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/token-fields), [Edit menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/edit-menus), [The menu bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar)
- **Context-preserving overlays versus the action budget:** HIG often favors a picker near its field, supplementary nonmodal panes, or a popover that leaves its source visible; a small overlay may put several actions into the same backend cells or keep background actions live. Preserve context only within the budget; an explicit temporary task screen is a proposal when the overlay cannot fit. [Pickers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/pickers), [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets), [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers), [Panels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/panels)
- **Dynamic menu reversal, hiding, and movement:** HIG allows context menus to reverse order around their invocation point and hide unavailable items, whereas menu bars retain disabled items. Such changes can remap a gaze quadrant mid-selection. Choose a stable mapping and explicit transition behavior rather than treating either platform convention as universally correct. [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus), [The menu bar](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/the-menu-bar)
- **Gesture-only dismissal and resizing:** outside click, swiping, grabber dragging, and detent changes are not sufficient gaze exits and can change quadrant ownership. Supply an explicit valid exit and preserve work; never treat looking outside a view as Apple's click-outside confirmation. [Sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sheets), [Popovers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/popovers)
- **Destructive order and defaults are context-specific:** action sheets favor destructive choices at the top, context menus put them last, button guidance rejects a destructive primary role, and alert guidance allows a Return-default exception after deliberate destructive intent. Do not collapse these into a universal order or use the exception to auto-confirm a gaze choice. [Action sheets](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/action-sheets), [Context menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/context-menus), [Buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons), [Alerts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/alerts)
- **Native editing versus accessible repair controls:** HIG discourages duplicating edit-menu controls, but an OS edit menu cannot be the sole path for a gaze-only user. Preserve a usable Clarte repair route; avoid showing duplicate actions in the same state. Copyable text must not become multiple unbudgeted word targets. [Edit menus](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/edit-menus), [Labels](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/labels)
- **Automatic updates versus selection stability:** HIG recommends live control updates and content refresh, but changes that replace a dwell target or reorder predictions need separate gaze-safety handling. Update the reported state truthfully without silently changing the action being selected. [Controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/controls), [Progress indicators](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/progress-indicators), [Token fields](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/token-fields)
- **Quick posting versus deliberate communication:** activity-extension examples minimize steps and can post immediately; that is not authorization to auto-speak or auto-share Clarte's predicted text. Preserve deliberate release and distinguish a chooser closing, an operation starting, and successful delivery. [Activity views](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/activity-views)
