# Style 6 · Orb

The owner's concept of 2026-11-07, built on style 5 · Dialogue. The grid stays where style 5 put it. The conversation screen gets one object: a gradient orb in the centre rest zone, which holds the message like the jewel in the owner's reference, reacts to the conversation and leans towards whatever card the person is looking at. Two words that fit the typed groups equally well branch into two choices. The first run is a three-page setup in the look of a data report. Every word is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next.

The second round of references (owner, 2026-11-07, three Pinterest pins) made it simpler: frosted glass cards with a plain icon and words at the lower left that turn white as they are looked at, a light-orange orb, the menu opening over the blurred conversation, and the word in progress drawn as a selected object with two handles. The owner then took the page back to plain white: the orb's light orange is the only orange on the screen.

The always-on word and always-on listening (owner, 2026-11-07) changed what the two top cards do, not where anything is: Q1 is always the predicted word (or Options, when several whole words fit the code), Q4 is whatever is most needed at that moment, the microphone runs whenever the device is not speaking, and the conversation screen is wired to the prediction engine (`prototypes/Word Prediction/v1/CONTRACT.md`) with a local fallback. The Phrases page, the "One moment" floor-holder, the Listen button and the stand-in whole-reply suggestion are gone.

The third round (2026-11-07) put it on the real engine (`prototypes/Word Prediction/v1`), found by its health rather than by an open port, with the local decoder standing in while the engine warms or is away; added **Other words** to the Done page, a chooser that lists every exact-length word for the last word's code four at a time; and hardened the screen against a hostile demo (`scripts/hostile.mjs`, fifteen cases, each ending with a word in Q1 and no error, loading text or blank card).

The fourth round (2026-11-08) answered an independent hostile demo review: a dwell now runs exactly what its card showed (no dead dwell when the engine answers late), the word just accepted is never offered again, the card holds its word while the partner speaks instead of flickering, names keep the case the engine gives them (`display`), three quick presses on Q4 can no longer speak, Recalibrate has a gaze-reachable exit and comes back to the message, the local fallback echoes the partner's words and reads as English, a 20 px type floor holds at every size including browser zoom, the newest partner turn keeps two lines at 200%, unavailable cards and pager dots keep 3:1, and the setup copy follows `minimal-ui`. `scripts/hostile.mjs` has a case for each (17–31).

Everything else (the thread, the dwell indicator, the guards, calibration, the menu) is style 5's. Its decisions are in [../s5/DESIGN.md](../s5/DESIGN.md) and are not repeated here.

## Quadrants

Conversation: exactly style 5's grid. Q1 is the ink card, Q4 the white card, Q5–Q8 the letter groups (live in every state except speaking), and Q2–Q3 one rest zone with the thread, the message and the orb in every state.

| State | Q1 `primary` | Q4 `secondary` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Message empty (the first word too, and while the partner is being heard) | the next word | Menu |
| Typing (groups pending) | the completion | Delete (last group) |
| Two close whole words (the branch) | the first word | the second word |
| Reopened (after Delete word) | the next candidate | Delete word (a second Delete removes it) |
| Message has words, nothing pending | the next word | **Done** |
| Speaking | Stop | unavailable (Menu, dimmed and named) |
| Just spoken (6 s, held while gaze rests on it) | the next word | Say again |

Q1 (`src/policy.ts`, `primaryFor`): the engine's top word for the conversation as it stands, except the word just accepted, which is never offered as the next word (the next distinct word takes its place, `nextWordIndex`; the engine and the local decoder suppress it too, this is the screen's own guard against "hello hello"). Accepting it appends exactly the word shown when the dwell completed: `useSettled` holds a label under a resting gaze, and the activation runs the function the settled label shows (round 4; it used to compare the label with the live prediction and drop the activation when a late engine answer had arrived underneath, which was a dead dwell in 5 of 10 looks that started within 60 ms of a new turn and in every look when the engine took over 400 ms; `scripts/hostile.mjs` case 18 now measures 0, 300, 700 and 3000 ms). A late engine answer can replace a label only once gaze has left its card. The engine hears of each accepted word (`/api/v1/commit`). The message shows the word in progress only while groups are pending; with nothing typed the next word stays on its card, so the sentence the partner reads is what was said, not a guess.

Q4 (`secondaryFor`) is one pure function over state and prediction, in that priority order, tested branch by branch in `scripts/test-policy.ts` (`node scripts/test-policy.ts`). A label changes only when gaze is not resting on the card, and never under an armed dwell; a dwell that completes on "Delete" deletes, whatever the engine answered meanwhile. A key or click gets the same leave-and-return guard as a dwell for anything that speaks: Speak (and Say again) in a quadrant where a different action was just activated waits for the pointer to leave that quadrant, or 400 ms by key, so three quick presses on Q4 never go Delete → Done → Speak and talk (case 25).

**Done** opens a page in the menu geometry: Q1 Back to the message, Q2–Q3 the message as it stands (the thread does not change, only the cards), Q4 **Speak** in the spot Done occupied, Q5–Q8 Delete word, Clear message, Other words and Recalibrate. Recalibrate (here and in the Menu) remembers where it was opened from (`screen.from`) and returns there, with the message as it was: to the Done page, to the conversation, or to the setup report on the first run; it used to land on the setup report and two more pages whatever `?onboarding=off` said. Calibration itself is style 5's simulated sequence (`src/Calibration.tsx`, not shared with `prototypes/Eye Tracking`, whose real calibration lives in its own `src/core/calibration.ts`); the run takes about 44 s, so it has a gaze-reachable exit that respects the quadrant rule: a short **Back** card in Q1, below the corner target and above the middle-left one so no target is covered, live whenever no target is in Q1 and unavailable (no action, dimmed) while the guide travels to or rests on a Q1 target, since a look that follows the dot there must never count as a dwell on Back; when it comes back the quadrant guard asks the gaze to leave and return. Escape leaves too. Back and Escape leave without marking calibration done; Start on the Ready step marks it done (cases 21 and 22). Looking twice at Q4 speaks: the first look opens the page, and Speak needs gaze to leave the card and return (the engine's latch, the quadrant guard and the owner-change guard all hold it), so speech never fires from one look at a card that was Delete or Done a moment before. Delete word reopens the last word at the next candidate and returns to the message; Delete word again, after accepting, pages on through the engine's ranking (`offset`), so every candidate is reachable. Recalibrate was kept over Settings because it is the direct repair when selection gets hard mid-message; Settings is one step away through Menu once the message is spoken or cleared, and Menu's third item, Demo, is not needed with a message open.

**Other words** (round 3) took Settings' place on the Done page. A rare word is often deep in the engine's list (its coverage run: 49% of dictionary words are not top-2 at the full code with no context), and Delete word reaches rank r only after 2r + 1 looks. Other words reopens the last word's own code and asks the engine for `complete: true` words (exact length only, so a 2-group code lists two-letter words, never their completions), three per page in Q5–Q7, with Q1 Back and a right arrow in Q8 that swaps in the next three (owner, 2026-11-08). The arrow card reads "Page 2 of 4"; on the last page it is gone, so the end is plain, and Back leaves. Q4 is Spell (below). The message stays in Q2–Q3 with the word about to give way drawn as the selected object (the same box and handles as the word in progress). Picking a word replaces the last word, tells the engine (`commit`) and returns to the message; the word remembers its rank, so Delete word pages on from there. A page with fewer than four words leaves the rest of the row as rest zones; one page has no More. Cost in looks: rank r costs 3 + ⌊r/4⌋ (Done, Other words, More × pages, the word), against 2r + 1 by Delete word, so it wins from rank 2 on. With the local decoder the pages are its exact-length words; with an engine that does not yet honour `complete`, its best 64 are sifted for exact words and the local list follows them. A chooser page is read as a whole, so the list that first appeared stays until the page is left, even if a better answer arrives late.

Menu (message empty): Q1 Back; Settings, Recalibrate, Demo in Q5–Q7; settings pages with More in Q4; the Demo page's fourth card, **Partner speaks**, plays the next scripted partner line.

## Always listening (`src/voice.ts`, `hearPartner`)

There is no Listen button. With the microphone on (Demo → Microphone), speech recognition runs on every conversation screen whenever the device is not speaking: it is paused while speaking, so the device never captions itself, and restarted when speech ends. It never stops on a timer: Chrome ends a continuous session on silence and every ended session restarts. The words being heard show in the thread (the live line while the message is empty, the newest turn once typing has started) and go to the engine as the last partner turn; a phrase the recogniser finalises, or words still interim when a session ends (a pause), become a partner turn. Taking the turn (Speak) keeps whatever was being heard as the partner's turn first. Errors (no permission, offline) back off from 1 s to 30 s and keep trying quietly; there is no scripted fallback, because a partner who did not speak must never appear to. Typing works at all times except while speaking.

The state pill reads Speaking; Listening while the partner's words arrive; Replying as soon as a reply is owed or a draft exists; Listening again when the microphone is on and nothing else is happening; nothing when the microphone is off and the thread is quiet.

The scripted partner (demos and automated checks, which have no microphone): Demo → Partner speaks plays the next of the four demo lines word by word as the live transcript, then commits it as a turn; `window.clarteDebug.partner(text?)` does the same.

## The word (`src/prediction.ts`, `src/local.ts`)

**Finding the engine.** An engine is a server whose `GET /api/v1/health` answers `{ ok: true, vocabulary: n }`; an open port is not enough (the owner's research server also sits on 8765). `?engine=<origin>` names one candidate; otherwise 8765 and the four ports after it are probed. Until one answers, and whenever the one in use stops answering (two failed requests in a row), the local decoder stands in and the probe repeats every two seconds, so a cold engine (about 30 s) is picked up the moment it binds; the answers the local decoder gave are then forgotten and the next request is the engine's. `?engine=off` keeps the local decoder only. The switcher (`s_all/serve.mjs`) does the same check: when 8765 is held by something that is not the engine it starts v1 on the next free port and tells S6 through `GET /engine.json`, which the switcher page turns into S6's `?engine=` parameter.

**Answering.** Every request to the engine carries the whole conversation (every turn, plus the words being heard as the last partner turn), the accepted words, the typed groups and the page offset, and an id; a response that is not the latest for its request is dropped, as is anything malformed (a 500, HTML where JSON was expected, a hang past 4 s). The engine is raced against a 250 ms clock and the local decoder: a slow, broken or unreachable engine never shows as a spinner, "loading" or a blank card, the local word simply stands. The engine's answer takes the local one's place when it arrives, and a label changes only once gaze has left its card (`useSettled`), never under a resting look or an armed dwell; the hold starts from the first gaze sample on the card, before the gaze engine's 150 ms quadrant hold (`useLooked`), so an answer that lands in that window waits too; an activation always takes the word that was shown. While the partner is speaking, Q1 relabels only when the engine's new word is clearly better and at most every 4 s from the start of the turn (`HEARING_RELABEL_MS`), so it does not flicker with each word heard. The next word is prefetched behind the word on screen, so accepting it never waits.

**While the partner speaks** (round 4; the card used to relabel 22 times in one 8.5 s turn, alternating the local "I" with the engine's "Yes" and "What"): the words being heard reach the engine at most every 350 ms (`useThrottled`); for the same message and typed groups the engine's standing word is kept until the engine answers, never swapped for a local word while the engine is healthy; and a new engine word replaces the shown one only when it is clearly better, 1.5 × the shown word's probability in the new answer (`usePrediction`). The turn's final answer shows once the partner stops. Case 23 counts the relabels during the long scripted line: at most two.

**`display`** (round 3 of the contract): a word may come with its written form ("I", "I'm", "Priya" as the partner wrote it); the card, the word in progress, the message and the spoken text use it (`text.ts`, `displayWord`), and the accepted word remembers it (`model.ts`, `ShownWord`). Without it the old rule holds: "i" is "I" and the first word takes a capital. A `corrected` word on the first page (a probable gaze slip, round 3) shows where it ranks and is marked a slip like the fallback's correction.

**The branch** is the engine's call (`branch: true`, round 3 of the contract: both words complete, p₁ / p₀ ≥ 0.3, p₀ ≥ 0.2): Q4 offers `words[1]`. The local decoder, and an engine that does not say, use the rule in `conflict.ts`, kept as strict: the two best words both whole, neither a correction, p₀ ≥ 0.2, p₁ / p₀ ≥ 0.6 and the pair three times the mass of longer words. The screen never hides Delete on its own: a late `branch` arriving while gaze rests on Delete waits until gaze leaves, and the lines are drawn from the settled labels, so they never point at a card that still reads Delete (case 19).

**Nothing matches.** When the typed groups match no word, Q1 still shows one: the engine's top correction (one digit off, a gaze slip), else the local decoder's best word for the longest matching prefix. In the message, a letter from another group than the one typed at its place takes its own group's colour with a dotted underline, so the colour says where the look went astray; groups typed past the word show as dots and may wrap. Accepting such a word stores the word's own code, so Delete word and Other words reopen what was said.

The local decoder (`src/local.ts`) is the engine's own vocabulary (`src/lexicon.json`, about 23k words with unigram frequencies, generated by `scripts/build-lexicon.mjs` from `Word Prediction/v0/models/vocabulary.json`) and four tables read off the engine's ARPA 4-gram by the scripts beside it (round 4): the best eight next words of the 3,000 most frequent previous words and of the sentence start (`bigrams.json`, `build-bigrams.mjs`); the best four next words of about 11,000 two-word histories, the most frequent predecessors of each of the 400 most frequent words with a wider window for hubs such as "to" and "not" (`trigrams.json`, `build-trigrams.mjs`); for each of the most frequent content words, p(word | function word) for the 250 most frequent words that the model has seen before it (`echo.json`, `build-echo.mjs`); and how readily a sentence ends after each frequent word (`ends.json`, `build-ends.mjs`). It answers in the engine's shape (`p`, `complete`, `total`, `longer`, `display` for a name the partner wrote with a capital), including `complete: true` pages.

Ranking follows the engine's own recipe as far as n-gram tables allow (`v1/engine/cache.py`): the language model's p(word | the two words before it), else p(word | the word before) from the bigram or echo table, else the unigram scaled down; mixed with a pointer cache over the conversation (the partner's words at 1.0, the person's at 0.5, decaying 0.7 per turn, names doubled, damped for frequent words so "cold" counts and "you" does not), at weight 0.12 while typing and 0.04 for the next word. Table-known continuations come first; a partner's content word joins them at every rank while typing, and as the next word only where the echo table finds it at least five times likelier after the word before it than in general ("not cold", never "I cold" or "am coffee"). The next word is nudged towards words a sentence can end on, so a tie in the model ("I am not" → to, a, sure, so within 0.04 log) resolves to "I am not sure". The word just accepted is never the next word and every word already in the message keeps a tenth of its mass, so a chain never loops.

What that gives, measured by `scripts/test-policy.ts` and `test-local.ts` on the fallback alone: typing "te" after "Do you want tea or coffee?" offers tea (it used to offer You → Was → Year), "co" offers coffee, "c" after "I am" and "Are you cold?" offers cold, "pr" after "Your sister Priya is coming" offers Priya with its capital; the eight-word chains are "I am not cold and windy today is" for "Are you cold?", "I am not sure if you don't have" for the tea question and most others, "I am not tonight and tomorrow morning to" for the dinner question, every step a known continuation or the partner's own word, no word repeated twice. The chains are plain and sometimes alike: this is the fallback, the engine is the primary path, and the partner's words are one or two letters away on it.

## The orb (`src/Orb.tsx`)

One WebGL fragment shader on a canvas behind the thread. It has no library and no texture, and renders at 0.6× device pixels because the orb is soft anyway.

- **Shape.** A circle whose radius is modulated by three low harmonics (2, 3 and 5 lobes) drifting at different rates. That gives the Lottie's slow organic loop: about one full change of shape every 24 s, or 0.04 Hz. That is well under the 0.2 Hz the HIG says people are most sensitive to.
- **Fill.** Light orange (owner, 2026-11-07), drawn at 60% opacity on the white page so it reads as a tint (one token, `--orb-opacity`; the dark theme keeps 100%). Four colour points, from light orange to near white, blended by inverse distance drift inside the shape. The rim towards the lower left lifts to white, like the health app's orb. The core is slightly deeper, the edge is soft but defined, and a faint halo in the orb's own colour surrounds it.
- **Mood.** The conversation state gives the orb a theme within orange: idle is peach, Listening leans coral, Replying apricot (its own theme, owner 2026-11-07) and Speaking green (owner, 2026-11-07), all light. The theme cross-fades over 600 ms. The state pill stays the text twin, so colour is never the only cue.
- **Lean** (owner, 2026-11-07). The orb leans towards the card gaze is on: a letter group, a word choice, Delete on the right, a menu item, anything with an action. The body shifts about 45 px that way and bulges on that side. It aims at the card's centre, never at the raw gaze point, so it is not a cursor to chase. It eases there at its own unhurried pace (time constant 1.2 s, so a 600 ms letter dwell is over before it arrives) and drifts back to centre when gaze returns to a rest zone. The wobble keeps the same speed throughout. Evidence: `evidence/lean-rest-delete-g1-g4.png` shows rest, Delete, A–F and T–Z after 3.5 s.
- **Speaking** (owner, 2026-11-07). From Speak until the speech ends, the orb is livelier: an energy value eases in over 0.5 s, the slow lobes deepen by 80%, the wobble runs about 2.4× faster (still under 0.2 Hz), a quick seven-lobe ripple runs round the edge, and it is drawn at 80% opacity instead of 60%. The green is the lightest of the four moods. When speech ends or is stopped it calms back over 0.9 s. Measured in the browser, the orb's pixels change about 4× as much per 400 ms while speaking as at rest (the motion is the same as before; the paler green shows less of it).
- **Pulse.** Each new word heard from the partner swells the orb by 3.5%, and each spoken word by up to 7%, then it settles on a spring (Motion).
- **Off.** Reduced motion, or Menu → Settings → Orb motion: Still, keeps one shape and never leans. Only the theme colour (and Speaking's stronger opacity) still cross-fades.
- **Luminance.** Each palette's deepest orange has relative luminance 0.58 to 0.66, and moods shift hue, not lightness. At 60% opacity the deepest point is lighter still, so these are minimums: on it ink keeps at least 10:1, secondary ink 5.5:1, ghost letters 3.4:1, and the typed group inks 3.8:1 or more (52 px text).
- **Cost.** The frame loop stops whenever nothing is changing: when the orb is set to Still and settled, or when the tab is hidden.

Each turn in the history shows who said it (owner, 2026-11-07): a microphone at the left of each turn heard from the partner, and a speaker at the right of each reply the person spoke, in the quiet ink at the turn's text size. The icon floats beside the text, so a long turn that is shortened keeps its ellipsis and never runs into the icon. Screen readers hear "Heard:" or "Said:" before the turn.

The live message is centred on the orb. History keeps style 5's left and right alignment above it, and the hairline is gone because the orb is the frame.

## The branch (`src/conflict.ts`, `src/Branch.tsx`)

When the typed groups spell two whole words about equally well, the screen offers both instead of guessing. Q1 shows the first word, as it always does. Q4 shows the second word in place of Delete. The word in progress is drawn as a selected object: a hairline box with a handle at each end (the editor reference). Two lines grow from the handles, the left to Q1 and the right to Q4. Each drops below the word, runs beneath the message and rises into a port on the card's inner edge.

**When it branches.** This is the contract for the language model:

- Both are whole words for the typed groups.
- p(second) / p(first) ≥ 0.6.
- p(first) + p(second) ≥ 3 × the summed probability of longer words with the same start.
- At least two groups have been typed, nothing has been reopened, and the engine is on its first page.

`p` and `longer` are read straight off the engine's answer (`conflict.ts`); the local decoder answers in the same shape.

The factor 3 was measured. Typing every prefix of all 1,256 vocabulary words, a factor of 1 gave 161 conflicts on whole words and 240 mid-word. A factor of 3 gave 98 and 38. Mid-word branches cost Delete for a look, so 3 it is. Example pairs: me/he, them/when, good/more, might/night, room/soon, well/tell.

**Motion.** A branch appears after a group is committed, a few dozen times a day, so each line draws once over 480 ms on the ease-out curve. A small head dot travels the line's front, and the end port fades in as it arrives. Nothing moves afterwards. The owner's note said the line "keeps moving until it reaches a new tracking point". Here it moves until it reaches the place to look, then stops, because a line that kept flowing while the person decides would be idle motion beside an armed target. Leaving is a 150 ms fade. Under reduced motion the finished drawing fades in.

**Recovery.** Typing another group continues the word and the branch goes. Delete is not reachable during a branch. To undo, accept either word, then Done → Delete word, which reopens it with the next candidate. A screen-reader status line says "Two words fit: might or night."

## Spell (`src/spell.ts`, owner 2026-11-08)

A word no dictionary has cannot come from the groups alone: "polyphiloprogenitive" is in none of wordfreq, Webster's 2nd or the New Oxford American Dictionary, so the engine returned nothing and Q1 showed "Private" (the best word for a seven-group prefix) with thirteen dots. When nothing matches and nothing is one slip away, Q1 now reads **Spell**. Spell walks the typed groups letter by letter: Q5–Q7 the three likeliest letters of the current group, the arrow in Q8 for the rest of that group, Q4 Delete (the last letter), Q1 Back (the groups as typed). The message shows the letters chosen and a dot per group still to spell. The last letter accepts the word, and the engine learns it (`commit`), so the next time it is offered whole.

Letters are ranked by a letter 4-gram over the stand-in lexicon's spellings (one count per word) with a two-letter look-ahead through the groups still to come. On 600 held-out words of six letters or more: 68% of letters first, 1.06 looks per letter; polyphiloprogenitive 22 looks for 20 letters, otorhinolaryngologist 25 for 21. A one-slip correction is still offered as a word.

Spell is also Q4 on both choosers (Options and Other words). With the engine's 866,000-word lexicon (round 4, Wiktionary) most codes match some dictionary word, so a new name or brand often has matches and Q1 never says Spell; from the chooser it is one look away. From Options it spells the word being typed; from Other words it respells the last word, which it then replaces. Back from that Spell returns to Other words. Hostile case 36 spells a surname both ways.

While a healthy engine is still answering, the local decoder's "nothing matches" is provisional (its lexicon is a fifth of the engine's): Q1 shows the unavailable "No word" until the engine answers, so Spell never opens for a word the engine knows. A local answer becomes final when the engine fails or is gone.

A long word in progress stays on one line so the message fit steps its type down; past twelve groups its dots are drawn smaller, so fifty groups fit one line at the 20 px floor, and the line keeps room for the selection box's handles. Hostile case 35 spells a new made-up word each run.

## Exact length first (`src/policy.ts` `EXACT_FIRST`, owner 2026-11-08)

While typing, Q1 prefers a word exactly as long as the groups typed over a longer completion when the whole word is at least 3% as likely (0.1 until round 4). The owner's case: "the" typed in full as the first word showed "What" (what 0.76, the 0.12, with no context to favour either). Completions still show while no whole word of that length is likely, so "w", "wh" still offer What. Replayed on the round-3 dev records: 2.9194 selections per word vs 2.9632 for completion first (1.5% fewer); showing only exact-length words, as first asked, would cost about 4.96, because no word could be finished early. Hostile case 33.

## Options (`src/conflict.ts` `optionsFor`, owner 2026-11-08)

When several whole words fit the typed groups and none is close enough to branch on, Q1 does not guess: it reads **Options** (an ellipsis icon) and Q4 stays Delete. The owner's case: "computer science is hosting" read as "growing"; both are 2334222, as are issuing and looting, and the engine is sure of neither branch. Options opens the word chooser for the code being typed: Q1 Back (to the word in progress, groups intact), Q5–Q7 the top three whole words, and the arrow in Q8 to the next three until none are left; a pick accepts the word and returns to the message. The word in progress shows as dots while Options stands, since no single word is on offer.

The rule: the word Q1 would show is a whole word, the engine did not branch, no gaze-slip correction counts, and either the top two words are both whole words with p(second) / p(first) ≥ 1e-4, or the next whole word below the one Q1 would show is at least 1% of it (`OPTIONS_NEXT`, round 4), or another whole word on the first page is at least 0.3 of the word Q1 would show (`OPTIONS_RIVAL`; owner, 2026-11-08: "the hero" showed "less", hero 0.047 against less 0.056 behind message and happy, so the top-two test never saw it). A rival anywhere on the page at 1e-4 would open Options on nearly every short word ("is" against go, no), so the loose ratio stays on the top two. The 0.3 clause is not replayed: the harness records keep only the top two words per typed length. The ratio is that low because the engine multiplies two context models and is overconfident (hosting/growing is 2.4e-4 after the full code). Replayed on the round-3 dev records, with the branch kept for close pairs: 2.9632 selections per word vs 2.9608 for the branch alone and 2.9832 with neither; Options shows in about 2% of typed states. Hostile cases 32 and 34 drive the owner's two sentences on the real engine.

## Every word, measured (round 4)

Two tools hold the claim "any word can be said" to numbers rather than intent. `scripts/oracle.mjs` is an oracle user: it says a target sentence through this screen and the real engine, taking whatever route the screen offers (Q1, Q4, Options, the arrow, Other words, Spell), and checks the message reads exactly the sentence and that no word cost more looks than spelling it from scratch. Its suites cover one-letter words to 45-letter words, a 38-word sentence, a 30-turn history, rare, medical and Wiktionary-only words, names said and unsaid, slang, contractions, repeats, invented words and profanity. `bench/policy_eval.py` (in `Word Prediction/v1`) replays screen policies over cached engine answers for 965 dev words and 2,655 word-contexts of every kind; `OPTIONS_NEXT` and `EXACT_FIRST` were chosen with it.

Found and fixed by them: Other words listed words of the accepted completion's spelling rather than the groups typed ("up" accepted as a longer word could not be found; `chooserCode` now uses the typed groups), and an accepted slip correction forgot the groups typed (`Word.typed`), so Spell could not reach "xyz". The remaining cost: a name or invented word nobody has said shares its code with some dictionary word, and the engine is sure of that word, so Q1 shows it and the person reaches Spell through Done → Other words, 3 looks more than a Spell in Q1 would cost. No signal in the engine's answer tells an unseen name from that word (an "unsure → Options" rule left names unchanged, `r4.policy`).

## Type and legibility

The typeface is **Atkinson Hyperlegible Next** (Braille Institute, 2025), bundled with `@fontsource-variable/atkinson-hyperlegible-next`, so no font request leaves the machine. It is designed for low-vision readers. Its letterforms are deliberately distinct (I l 1, O 0 with a slashed zero, rn m, b d p q), with open counters and a large x-height. Style 5 measured an x-height ratio of 0.496 for it before the shared skin moved to Satoshi.

Why this face for Clarte. Dysarthria is a motor speech disorder, but the people who have it often have the conditions that cause it: stroke, ALS, cerebral palsy, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's or brain injury. These often bring reduced acuity or contrast sensitivity, visual-field loss, nystagmus, fatigue or reading difficulty. Atkinson addresses letter confusion and low vision directly. OpenDyslexic was not chosen because studies have not found that it helps reading. Lexend is the alternative to try if the owner prefers a rounder face.

How it is set:

- Weights are 450 for reading text, 600 for messages and labels, and 650 for letter tiles and the state pill. Nothing uses a thin weight, and bold is used sparingly (the HIG prefers regular to bold).
- Tracking is never negative. Style 5's −0.025em was removed. Letter tiles are tracked +0.1em, so each letter stands apart.
- Line height is 1.16 for messages and 1.4 for thread turns.
- Text is sentence case, never all caps, with no italics.
- Nothing is smaller than 20 px (round 4: the strip's "1 min" and the units on the setup figures were 18 and 19.8 px). Card labels, setting values and the chooser's words are floored at 20 px in CSS and the fit pass (`fit.ts`) stops there; a word that still does not fit at the floor (a 22-letter word on a 128 px card) wraps inside itself with every letter visible rather than shrinking or being cut. The card geometry (gutters, edges, padding, icon) is fixed at 1280×720 and above and gives way below it, so browser zoom at 200% (a 1280×720 window is then 640×360 CSS px) keeps 128 px cards and 20 px type instead of 96 px cards and 4 px labels; case 28 checks it.
- Text size goes to 200% across the whole task. At 200% the newest partner turn keeps two whole lines (history type grows to 40 px at most, so two lines of it and a line of the message at its floor fit above the row boundary at 1280×720; it used to be clamped to "Where would you…"), and a message past its type floor shows whole lines only, its room clipped at the top to a whole number of lines rather than faded, so nothing is half-erased (cases 26 and 27).
- Secondary greys are darker than style 5's (`#3F3B45`, `#69646F`), so they pass on the orb as well as on the page.

## Look

- **White page** (owner, 2026-11-07: "back to white; only the gradient is light orange"). No frame, no wash. The orb is the only orange on the screen.
- **Glass cards.** Cards are frosted glass: a soft neutral veil (about `#F3F3F4` on white) with a hairline edge and a backdrop blur. Each control has a plain 36 px icon at its top left and its words at its bottom left. The two-circle motif and its ring are gone. The ink primary card in Q1 stays dark, so its luminance is constant.
- **The dwell is the card turning white.** After the intent delay the glass clears a step and its edge darkens (the focus cue). Then a white wash rises from the bottom edge, linearly over the remaining dwell, until the card is solid white. A selection adds an ink edge while gaze remains. A look that leaves early drains the wash over 150 ms, and after a selection it fades. The wash is one element scaled with `transform`, so a dwell does not paint. Under reduced motion it steps up in quarters. On the dark Q1 card the wash is a light veil instead of white. In confirm mode a preview shows the full wash at half strength.
- **Letter cards show only their letters** (owner, 2026-11-07), at the lower left in their group inks. Each letter card is a very light tint of its own group colour (8% of the group ink in white: pale blue, lavender, mint and sand), with an edge in the same hue that deepens on the focus cue; the look still fills it with white. The letters keep 5.8:1 or more on the tint. Style 5's count dashes were a colour-blind backup for group identity and repeated what each card already shows, so they are gone.
- **Delete is red** (`#D42A1E`, 4.6:1 on glass, a 36 px icon), as in the editor reference, and so is Clear message's bin. Only the icon is red; the label stays ink.
- **The state pill** is white glass with a coloured dot (red, magenta, green, as in style 5). Its width eases to each new label over 300 ms, like the reference dock.
- **The menu** opens over the conversation. The thread stays where it was and blurs (14 px, 300 ms) behind the menu's title, instead of being replaced.
- The selection edge and keyboard focus are ink, so nothing else competes with the orb's orange.
- Unavailable content (Q4 while speaking, Back while a calibration target is in Q1) is drawn at 50% (ink: 3.2:1 light, 4.6:1 dark, WCAG 1.4.11; it was 32%, under 2:1), the letter groups while speaking at 70% (their inks are lighter: 3.2:1 or more on their tints), and the pager's off dots and the setup counter's bars at 50% (round 4, case 29 measures them on the rendered page).
- Dark theme: near-black page, glass as a light veil, deep ember orb palettes, and light group inks.

## Libraries

- **Motion 14.6.0** (MIT) is used, in its imperative `animate()` form. It drives the orb's mood cross-fade, the stillness ease and the pulse spring, plus the branch's line draw. No React wrapper is needed. The card wash and the pill's width use the browser's Web Animations directly.
- **Custom WebGL** draws the orb. It is about 60 lines of GLSL, and it gives exact control over the two rules a library would not know: stop while gaze is on an action, and never change luminance.
- **Paper Shaders** (`@paper-design/shaders-react`, Apache-2.0) was evaluated and not used. Its mesh and grain gradients are the closest library match to the pastel reference, and it is the first thing to try if the owner wants a richer material. Its frame loop and parameters would need wrapping to freeze on gaze.
- **Lottie** (`lottie-web`, dotLottie) was not used. It plays authored vector animations. The reference blob would need re-authoring in orange, and it could not respond to state, freeze mid-loop or render a mesh gradient.
- **GSAP** (free, with all plugins) was not used. It could do everything above, but Motion already covers it at a smaller size.

## Departures from the contract

The owner's newest explicit decision wins (precedence 1, `docs/skills-for-clarte.md`). This style departs from the 2026-11-07 shared skin and the stillness rule in three places. Each departure is limited, so the others stay comparable.

1. **Gradients and glass.** The skin says "no gradients". This style has the orb and frosted (backdrop-blurred) cards.
2. **Idle motion.** The contract says "no idle motion" and "nothing moves while armed". The orb wobbles at 0.04 Hz all the time and leans slowly towards the looked-at card, even during a dwell. Mitigations: it is never attached to the gaze point, it stays in the rest zone, it moves at the same slow pace whatever the person does, and Orb motion: Still turns it off.
3. **Typeface.** The skin uses Satoshi. This style uses Atkinson Hyperlegible Next, which the voice prototype already uses.
